# Doc 03 — Academic Tracker & Texas Homeschool Compliance

**Purpose:** ground this system in what Texas law actually requires, define what "completing middle school" means for Shiloh specifically, and give Thomas a real evidence log to back up his own attestation as administrator.

---

## 1. The Legal Reality (read this first)

Texas is one of the least-regulated homeschool states in the country, and it's worth being precise about what that means, because it changes what this document is actually for.

- Under **TEA v. Leeper** (Texas Supreme Court, 1994), a homeschool is legally treated as a private school. Homeschools are exempt from compulsory public-school attendance law as a category, and the parent has the legal right to teach without needing anyone's permission.
- **There is no state registration, curriculum approval, or ongoing reporting requirement.** Nobody at TEA or a local district reviews or approves what Shiloh studies, and there is no formal state or district "board" that certifies grade-level completion for homeschoolers. Thomas, as administrator of what is legally Shiloh's private school, is the sole authority who determines and attests to grade level and completion. That's not a workaround — it's literally how Texas law is structured.
- **Required subject matter:** Texas law requires a "bona fide" (genuine, not fraudulent) course of study that includes five subjects: **reading, spelling, grammar, mathematics, and good citizenship**, delivered in visual form (books, workbooks, video, etc.). There is no state-mandated subject list beyond these five, no mandated hours, no mandated number of school days, and no state-mandated testing.
- **If withdrawing from public school**, the only requirement is a written notice of withdrawal stating the homeschool start date — no additional compliance paperwork.
- **Transcripts** are not legally mandated but are the practical tool homeschoolers use to document a student's record for anything downstream — re-enrollment in public/private school, dual credit, college admission, athletic eligibility, or military enlistment. A solid transcript documents: courses completed, grades, GPA, any standardized test scores, credits earned, and (eventually) graduation date.

**What this means in plain terms:** there is no external body Thomas needs to satisfy. The "attestation" this project talks about is Thomas's own attestation, as Shiloh's private-school administrator, to Thomas's own standard — and the evidence log below exists so that attestation is backed by something real, not just Thomas's say-so, in case it's ever needed by a future school, program, or institution.

*(Sources reviewed: Texas Home School Coalition (thsc.org) requirements summary, cross-referenced against general 2026 Texas homeschool law guides. If Thomas is ever navigating a specific edge case — re-enrollment cutoffs, a specific district's transfer policy, NCAA eligibility, financial aid — that's worth a direct check with THSC or a Texas homeschool attorney rather than relying on this doc alone, since individual district/institution policies can vary even where state law doesn't require them to.)*

## 2. Shiloh's Actual Academic History (as told, for the record)

This section exists so the "why" behind the plan below isn't lost over time.

- Primarily homeschooled through early elementary.
- Held back / repeated 4th grade after starting behind following the homeschool years.
- Homeschooled again for a period.
- Enrolled in public school for 6th grade.
- Enrolled in public school for approximately 3/4 of 7th grade — struggled significantly, borderline failing most classes. Teachers liked him personally and had no issue with his conduct or character; the failure pattern was academic, not behavioral or relational.
- Root cause, per Thomas's own read of the situation (and the working assumption this whole system is built around): Shiloh started 7th grade already behind, took on a course load that was too difficult given that starting point, got overwhelmed early, and never developed a working system for managing multiple assignments, deadlines, and test prep. The gap widened because of a missing *process* skill, not a missing *ability* — and by several accounts, the resulting sense of falling behind became its own weight ("ego perception") on top of the academic gap itself.
- Chronological age (14) would place him entering 9th grade this fall in a traditional system. Academically, he does not have a completed record for 7th or 8th grade.

## 6. Gap-Closing Plan (the actual point of this whole document)

Given the root cause identified in Section 2, the plan is explicitly **not** "redo 7th grade content harder." It's:

1. **Build the system first, load content into it second.** Before piling on subject content, establish one working planner/tracker ritual (the Monday/daily/Friday rhythm in [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]) and prove it holds for 2–3 weeks with a light course load.
2. **Right-size the load.** Fewer subjects moving at a real pace beats a full course load repeating the overwhelm pattern. Ramp up only once the system is holding.
3. **Make the system visible and shared**, not something Shiloh is expected to have internalized silently — Thomas reviews it weekly, out loud, without it feeling like surveillance (this is a coaching relationship, per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 1, not a compliance check).
4. **Route content through his real interests where possible.** Sports/fitness and business/entrepreneurship are his strongest pulls right now — math, reading, and writing all have natural homes inside both (stats and training logs for sports; budgeting, pricing, and contracts for business). Motivation follows relevance.
5. **Treat "borderline failing" as data, not a verdict.** The record starts now. Nothing about the 7th-grade transcript needs to follow him forward if the current plan produces a real, evidenced 7th/8th-grade-equivalent record.
