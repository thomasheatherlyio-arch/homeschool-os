# Bank — Executive function

**Status: draft** (pending Dad's approval of [[20261122-readiness-examiner]]). **Items: 12.** Scoring notes live only in [[key-executive-function]]. Do not open the key during a sitting.

**Strands:** starting a task; planning a short block of work; estimating time; organizing materials; handling distraction; shifting after a setback; working memory on a short multi-step direction; ending a session and leaving a next step. Items are self-report or short scenarios. There is no single correct personality, and this bank is not a character test or a medical screening.

Answer honestly on self-report items. For scenarios, give a plan someone could actually follow in the next 15–25 minutes.

### E1 (easy, self-report) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
Think of a school task you finished in the last two weeks (Khan, writing, a chore with a due time, or a lesson). In three to five sentences, how did you start it, and what helped you keep going past the first two minutes?

### E2 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10A (planning)
You have a 25-minute block and one goal: finish a 10-problem mixed review and write down the two you want to ask about. Sketch a minute-by-minute or staged plan (start, work, check, end). Include when you will write the two questions.

### E3 (medium, self-report) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
On a 1–5 scale, how accurate is your time estimate for a homework block you have not done before (1 = I usually finish much earlier or later than I thought; 5 = I am usually close)? Give one recent example, then one tactic that would make the next estimate better.

### E4 (medium, scenario) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
It is 11:05. You have until 11:25 for a sitting or a short assignment. Your desk has yesterday's papers, a charging cable, and a water bottle you meant to refill. What do you do in the **first two minutes**, and what do you deliberately *not* do, so the work can start?

### E5 (medium, scenario) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
You sit down to work and a notification, a sibling, or a tempting tab appears. Write a if-then plan in this form: "If [distraction], then I will [action] , and I will return to [the exact next step of the work]." The action should be usable the same day, not "I will become a more focused person."

### E6 (easy, multiple choice) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
You are in the middle of a 10-step direction for a lab or a worksheet. The best working-memory habit is:
- A. Try to hold all ten steps in your head and start with step 7 if it looks more fun
- B. Glance once, close the paper, and guess
- C. Keep the directions visible, mark the step you are on, and check the list before moving on
- D. Ask someone else to do the middle steps so you only do the last one

### E7 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10A
Tomorrow you have two short jobs (15 minutes of math review and a paragraph to revise) and one longer job that will not finish tomorrow (a history reading). Write an order for the afternoon and one sentence about why the longer job still gets a start or a next-step note even if it cannot be finished.

### E8 (hard, scenario) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
You planned to finish a worksheet. After eight minutes you see that two problems are harder than you thought and you feel like quitting or skipping the rest. What are two workable options for the remaining 15 minutes that still leave a recorded next step (for you or Dad)? Which option would you choose today, and why?

### E9 (medium, self-report) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
How do you usually leave a work session so tomorrow-you can start without searching? Mention materials, a written next step, or both. If you usually leave things scattered, say so honestly and write one change you will try once this week.

### E10 (medium, scenario) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
A plan said "do science, then write the paragraph." The science took the whole block. At the end of the block, what do you write down before you stand up? Include: what got done, what did not, and the first action for the next block.

### E11 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10A / study skills
You will have three 25-minute blocks tomorrow: one for a subject that feels easy, one for a subject that usually stalls, and one flex block. Propose a sequence and a rule for the flex block (when you would use it for the stalled subject vs. a break or a third task). Give a reason that is about energy and startup, not about "being good."

### E12 (easy, self-report) — no specific TEKS; 9th-grade-entry study skills
Name one tool you already use to remember a next step (notebook, calendar, timer, a list, asking someone to check). Then write the exact next-step sentence you would leave yourself tonight for whatever work is actually open.
