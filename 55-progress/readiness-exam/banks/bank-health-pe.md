# Bank — Health & PE

**Status: draft** (pending Dad's approval of [[20261122-readiness-examiner]]). **Items: 12.** Scoring notes live only in [[key-health-pe]]. Do not open the key during a sitting.

**Strands:** fitness principles and training (8.8); reading a simple activity or fitness log (8.9); nutrition and hydration for activity (8.10); safety in movement and play (8.11); social-emotional skills in games and groups (8.12–8.15); lifetime wellness habits (8.16). Items are self-report or short scenarios. There is often no single "correct personality." Scorers look for a complete, safe, workable plan — not a medical answer.

Do not invent injuries, diagnoses, or treatments. If a scenario mentions discomfort, stay with rest, telling a trusted adult, and stopping unsafe activity.

### P1 (easy, self-report) — TEKS 8.16 / 8.8
In a typical weekday, about how many minutes do you spend in moderate or vigorous movement (walking with purpose, sports, lifting, a workout, active chores)? Name the main activity. Then name one barrier that sometimes gets in the way and one change you could actually try next week.

### P2 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.8A
In your own words, what is the difference between **skill-related** fitness (for example, agility or balance) and **health-related** fitness (for example, endurance or muscular strength)? Give one example activity that would train each.

### P3 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.8B / 8.8C
A student wants to get better at a 20-minute jog over the next four weeks. Using the idea of gradual progression (or FITT: frequency, intensity, time, type), write a simple, safe four-week outline. Do not prescribe a medical plan; keep it general (how often, about how long, how to increase a little).

### P4 (medium, self-report / constructed response) — TEKS 8.9A
Look at this sample log, then answer.

| Day | Activity | Minutes | How hard it felt (1 easy–10 all-out) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Brisk walk | 20 | 4 |
| Tue | — | 0 | — |
| Wed | Pickup basketball | 35 | 7 |
| Thu | Stretching | 10 | 2 |
| Fri | Brisk walk | 20 | 4 |

1. What pattern do you notice in the week?
2. If the goal is more consistent moderate activity, what is one specific change you would try the following week? Why?

### P5 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10A / 8.10B
A student has a 4:00 p.m. practice after school. Give two practical habits for food or water that support activity (for example, timing a snack, bringing water, not skipping lunch). Stay with ordinary everyday habits — no supplements, diets for a medical condition, or weight-loss plans.

### P6 (easy, multiple choice) — TEKS 8.11A
Before a pickup game in the driveway, which choice is the **safest** habit?
- A. Leave loose balls and a wet towel in the playing space so you can start faster
- B. Check the space for hazards, wear shoes that stay on, and agree on any out-of-bounds or "hold" calls
- C. Play through a sharp pain in a joint because stopping would look weak
- D. Start with all-out sprints and skip any warmup because the game is short

### P7 (medium, scenario) — TEKS 8.11B / 8.14
During a game, a classmate is clearly exhausted and not thinking clearly, but says, "I'm fine, keep going." What should you do first, and whom should you tell? Explain why "keeping the game going" is not the priority.

### P8 (medium, scenario) — TEKS 8.12 / 8.13
Your team is losing. One teammate starts blaming another in front of everyone. What is one thing you can say or do in the next minute that is respectful and keeps the group usable? What is one response that would make the climate worse?

### P9 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.9B, 8.8
Using the log in P4, write two sentences a coach could use to describe the week without judging the person. Then name one fitness component (endurance, strength, flexibility, or skill) the week trained **most**, and one it barely trained, with a reason.

### P10 (medium, self-report) — TEKS 8.15 / 8.16
Name one way you recover after hard activity (sleep, a quieter evening, light movement, or something else you actually do). Then name one sign that you probably need more recovery rather than another hard session the next day. Do not describe medical symptoms or treatments; stay with ordinary energy, mood, and soreness you can manage by rest and telling an adult if needed.

### P11 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10C / 8.16
Why does water matter during a long practice on a hot day? Give a practical rule you could follow (when you would drink, or what you would bring) without using medical claims.

### P12 (hard, scenario) — TEKS 8.14, 8.16
A friend wants you to try a challenge video that includes a stunt on concrete with no pads. They say everyone is doing it. Write a short plan for how you would decide (yes, no, or a safer version): what risk you would consider, one question you would ask, and one sentence you could say out loud if you decline.
