# Bank — Science

**Status: draft** (pending Dad's approval of [[20261122-readiness-examiner]]). **Items: 12.** Answers live only in [[key-science]]. Do not open the key during a sitting.

**Strands:** matter and chemical vs. physical change (8.6); force and motion / Newton's laws (8.7); waves (8.8); the universe and the HR diagram / light (8.9); weather (8.10); climate and the carbon cycle (8.11); ecosystems (8.12); cells, heredity, and adaptations (8.13).

Use complete sentences for constructed response. Name the science idea, not only a guess.

### S1 (easy, multiple choice) — TEKS 8.6E / 8.6B
Which change is a **chemical** change?
- A. Ice melting in a glass
- B. Water boiling on a stove
- C. A raw egg becoming a cooked egg in a hot pan
- D. Tearing a sheet of paper in half

### S2 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.6E
How can you tell a chemical change from a physical change? Give one example of each (you may use everyday examples).

### S3 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.7A / 8.7B
A loaded wagon is harder to start moving than an empty wagon, and once it is moving it is harder to stop. Which of Newton's laws helps explain both observations? Explain in terms of mass, force, and acceleration or inertia. (You may state the law in your own words.)

### S4 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.7C
A skateboarder pushes backward on the ground and the skateboard moves forward. Use Newton's third law to explain why. Identify the pair of forces.

### S5 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.8A / 8.8B
A sound wave and a light wave both carry energy. Name one important way they are different (for example, what they travel through, or whether they are mechanical). Then explain why you can hear a radio in another room even when you cannot see the speaker.

### S6 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.9A / 8.9B
Stars look like points of light, but they are not all the same. What does a star's luminosity and temperature (as shown on an H-R diagram) help astronomers compare? In one or two sentences, what is the Sun's rough place on that comparison (a main-sequence, average star — not the biggest or hottest)?

### S7 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.10A
Name two properties of air or water in the atmosphere that help cause weather (for example: temperature, humidity, air pressure, or wind). For one of them, explain how a change in that property can change the weather you feel.

### S8 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.11A / 8.11B
Carbon moves among the atmosphere, living things, and Earth systems. Give one everyday example of carbon moving **into** the atmosphere and one example of carbon being **taken up** from the atmosphere or stored. Keep the examples scientific (photosynthesis, combustion, respiration, or similar), not slogans.

### S9 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.12A / 8.12B
In a simple food chain — grass → grasshopper → frog → snake — what happens to the energy available to the snake compared with the energy in the grass? Why is that change expected in an ecosystem?

### S10 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.13B / 8.13C
A population of beetles lives on light-colored sand. Most beetles are tan; a few are dark. After many years the sand is still light, but a new bird that hunts by sight becomes common. What is a reasonable prediction about the beetle population over many generations, and what science idea (natural selection or adaptation of a population, not "the beetles tried to change") are you using?

### S11 (medium, multiple choice) — TEKS 8.13A
Which statement about cells is most accurate?
- A. Only animals have cells; plants are built from a different living material
- B. Cells are the basic units of structure and function in living organisms, and they contain structures that carry out jobs
- C. A whole organ is smaller than the cells that make it
- D. Heredity information is stored only in the bloodstream, never in cells

### S12 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.6C / 8.6D, 8.5 (scientific thinking)
A student claims, "If I mix vinegar and baking soda in an open cup and the mixture looks empty afterward, matter was destroyed." Is the claim scientifically sound? Explain what evidence you would want (mass, gas leaving the cup, or a closed container) and what conservation of mass would predict.
