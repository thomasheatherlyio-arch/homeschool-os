# Bank — Good Citizenship

**Status: draft** (pending Dad's approval of [[20261122-readiness-examiner]]). **Items: 12.** Answers live only in [[key-good-citizenship]]. Do not open the key during a sitting.

**Strands:** constitutional principles (8.15D); Bill of Rights and later amendments (8.16, 8.19B); unalienable rights and the Declaration (8.19A); responsible citizenship (8.19C); Federalists and Anti-Federalists (8.17); civic participation and points of view (8.21, 8.22). This bank is civic knowledge and judgment, not a character test.

Write short constructed answers in complete sentences unless the item is multiple choice.

### C1 (easy, multiple choice) — TEKS 8.19A
In the Declaration of Independence, "unalienable rights" are rights that:
- A. A king may cancel whenever it is convenient
- B. Belong to people by nature and cannot rightfully be taken away by government
- C. Apply only to people who own land
- D. Were invented after the Civil War

### C2 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.19A
The Declaration names life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness as unalienable rights. In your own words, what does *pursuit of happiness* mean as a civic idea — not as "getting whatever you want"?

### C3 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.15D
Name two principles of the U.S. Constitution (for example: limited government, republicanism, checks and balances, federalism, separation of powers, popular sovereignty, or individual rights). For each one, give a short, concrete example of how it works.

### C4 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.19B
Pick **one** protection from the Bill of Rights (the first ten amendments). State the protection, then explain in two or three sentences why it matters for ordinary people, not only for people in government.

### C5 (medium, multiple choice) — TEKS 8.16B / 8.16C
Which statement best describes why later amendments (after the Bill of Rights) matter for citizenship?
- A. They deleted the original Constitution and started over
- B. They have no legal force unless a state agrees each year
- C. They have extended or clarified rights and rules of participation (for example, ending slavery, defining citizenship, and expanding voting rights)
- D. They only change the names of the branches of government

### C6 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.17A
In one or two sentences each, what was a main Federalist argument for ratifying the Constitution, and what was a main Anti-Federalist concern? Then state one way the Bill of Rights is connected to that debate.

### C7 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.19C
List three examples of responsible citizenship that a student your age can actually do (not "run for president"). For one of them, explain how it helps a community, not just the individual.

### C8 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.21B / 8.22
A town is deciding whether to add a Saturday morning library hour. One neighbor wants it for students who work weekday afternoons; another worries about the extra cost. Write two fair questions you would ask before taking a side, and one fact you would want to know.

### C9 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.15D (checks and balances)
A student says, "If the president wants a law, it just happens." Correct that claim. Explain how a bill can fail even if a president supports it, using the idea of checks and balances or separation of powers.

### C10 (hard, constructed response) — TEKS 8.19C, 8.22
Read this short scenario, then answer.

> At lunch, two classmates argue about a school rule. One starts inventing rumors about the other to "win." A third student, Sam, is not in the argument but hears the rumors start.

What is one responsible civic choice Sam could make in the next ten minutes? What is one choice that would make the problem worse? Explain why your first choice is civic, not just "being nice."

### C11 (medium, constructed response) — TEKS 8.18A / 8.15E
Why do landmark Supreme Court cases matter to people who were not parties in the case? Give a short answer in your own words. (You do not have to name a specific case, but you may.)

### C12 (easy, constructed response) — TEKS 8.19B / 8.15D
What is the difference between a **right** and a **responsibility**? Give one example of each from civic life.
