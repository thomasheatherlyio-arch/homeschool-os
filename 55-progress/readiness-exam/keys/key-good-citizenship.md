# Key — Good Citizenship (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

**Scoring:** C1 and C5 = 1 or 0. All other items = 2 complete and accurate, 1 partial, 0 blank or contradicted by civic fact. This is a knowledge-and-judgment bank, not a character grade. **Total: 22 points.**

### C1 — **Correct: B.**

### C2
A sound answer treats *pursuit of happiness* as the freedom to seek a good life, work, family, faith, or calling without government dictating a single path — within the law and without taking others' rights. "Do whatever you want" is too thin for a 2.

### C3
Two distinct principles, each with a concrete example (for example: checks and balances — Congress can pass a bill and the president can veto it; federalism — states run elections while the federal government handles enumerated powers; limited government — officials may only use powers the Constitution grants). 1 if examples are missing or the two principles are the same idea restated.

### C4
A named Bill of Rights protection (speech, press, religion, assembly, petition, bear arms, no unreasonable searches, due process, jury trial, etc.) plus a clear "why it matters for ordinary people." 1 if the protection is named but the explanation is only "it's important."

### C5 — **Correct: C.**

### C6
Federalist: a stronger national government (and the Constitution's design) was needed for union, defense, commerce, and/or to control factions. Anti-Federalist: fear of a too-powerful central government and/or the lack of an explicit list of rights. Connection: the Bill of Rights was added to protect individual liberties and help secure ratification. 1 if one side or the connection is missing.

### C7
Three age-appropriate actions (telling the truth in a report, serving on a student crew, volunteering, voting in a student election, showing up prepared, following just rules, informing yourself before sharing news, helping a neighbor, etc.). One of them must be tied to a community benefit. "Run for president" or illegal "vigilante" acts do not count.

### C8
Two fair, non-loaded questions (Who would use the hour? What would it cost? Who staffs it? What happens to other programs?) and one fact worth knowing (budget number, attendance estimate, hours already offered). 1 if the questions are only rhetorical attacks.

### C9
A president cannot make a law alone. A bill must pass both houses of Congress and then be signed (or a veto may be overridden by a two-thirds vote). Courts may also review a law later. A 2 names at least one real check; "other people stop him" without a branch or process is a 1.

### C10
A responsible choice: refuse to spread the rumor, tell the truth if asked, leave and get a trusted adult if it is escalating, or redirect to the actual disagreement about the rule. A worse choice: repeat the rumor, pile on, or record and post it. The civic reason should mention fairness, reputation, truth, or the community's ability to argue a rule without personal attacks — not only "kindness." Score completeness and civic reasoning, not Sam's personality.

### C11
Court decisions interpret the Constitution or laws; the rule they state can apply to later similar cases and to other people's rights. Naming a case (Marbury, McCulloch, Dred Scott, or a later familiar case) is a plus, not required.

### C12
A right is a protected freedom or claim (for example, free speech, due process). A responsibility is a duty of a citizen (for example, obeying just laws, serving on a jury when called, telling the truth under oath, respecting others' rights). Both example types needed for a 2.
