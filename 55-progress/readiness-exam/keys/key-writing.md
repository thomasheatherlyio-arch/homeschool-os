# Key — Writing (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

## Short-item scoring (W1–W9)

2 = complete and on-prompt; 1 = partial; 0 = blank or off-prompt. W7 is 1 or 0.

### W1
Audience: a classmate new to a kitchen scale. Purpose: explain how to use it (inform). Three steps/facts in a usable order (for example: put the bowl on the scale and tare; add the ingredient to the target weight; don't hold the bowl in the air). Deduct to 1 if audience or purpose is missing or the steps are jumbled.

### W2
Best order: (2) A twenty-five-minute timer can keep... [opening] → (4) For example, you might spend... [detail] → (1) The timer also makes it easier... [detail] → (3) Used this way, the timer is a tool... [close]. Allow a swap of the two middle detail sentences. 2 if order and labels are right; 1 if order is right but labels are missing or one pair is swapped badly.

### W3
A good revision names a specific morning action (for example, a 10-minute bag check, a short walk, or laying out tomorrow's materials), explains one clear benefit, and drops vague loops ("it helps you / it is important"). Do not require a particular activity.

### W4
Looks like an email (greeting, body, closing). Includes a request to move Tuesday's practice by thirty minutes, a reason, a proposed time, and a question. Tone is polite. 1 if a required part is missing or the tone is rude.

### W5
Two sentences, each with a concrete image or fact (fan, sucker rod, tank, wind, cattle trough, power outage, etc.). "The windmill is interesting" alone is not enough. Invented "example facts" are acceptable if labeled or clearly illustrative, not presented as researched statistics.

### W6
A clear yes or no claim; two reasons; one supporting fact or example tied to the first reason. 1 if the claim is mushy ("maybe") or a reason is missing.

### W7 — **Correct: B.**

### W8
Plan (not a full essay) with audience, purpose, three headed sections, and one detail under each. 1 if it is a full essay with no plan, or a plan missing sections.

### W9
Two clear sentences that keep all three ideas: rights, limits on government, and amendments. Filler ("stuff," "whatever") is gone. Example: "The Constitution matters because it protects individual rights and limits what the government may do. Amendments let the country change the document when those protections need to be extended or clarified."

## Longer-prompt rubric (W10–W12)

Score each longer piece **0–4** on three traits, then use the notes below. This is a sitting rubric, not a Doc 09a official level. Do not mark `verified`.

| Trait | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organization (8.10B(i)) | Clear opening, order, and close; transitions help | Structure is clear with a small slip | Some order, weak opening or close | Little visible structure | Blank / off-prompt |
| Development (8.10B(ii), 8.11) | Specific facts, steps, or images; prompt fully met | Mostly specific; one thin spot | General or one required part thin | Very thin or mostly repetition | No real content |
| Language and conventions (8.10C–D) | Clear sentences; errors do not block meaning | Readable; a few errors | Errors or vagueness sometimes block meaning | Hard to follow | Illegible or almost no sentences |

### W10
Must explain a real process the writer knows, with steps or parts, a caution or tip, and an assumed new reader. Invented expertise the writer could not have should be flagged, not rewarded.

### W11
Must take a position on flex time, use two reasons and one concrete example, and have some opening and close. A brief counterargument is a plus, not required for a 4 if the case is otherwise strong.

### W12
Must be a scene or narrative (not an essay of advice), show a second try, include a noticed detail, and have a beginning, turn, and ending.

Trait scores may be averaged or totaled (12 points possible per longer prompt) in the sitting note. Name one next 15–25 minute revision step in the action plan, not a character judgment.
