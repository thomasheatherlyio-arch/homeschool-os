# Key — Health & PE (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

**Scoring principle:** Self-report items (P1, P10, and the personal parts of P4/P5) have **no single correct personal answer**. Score completeness, safety, and a workable next step — not how athletic the student is, and not character. Never score "more minutes = higher grade" as if it were a fitness test. Unsafe advice (play through sharp pain, ignore a distressed classmate, attempt a concrete stunt to fit in) cannot receive a 2.

**Points:** P6 = 1 or 0. All other items = 2 complete and workable, 1 partial, 0 blank, off-prompt, or unsafe as the main plan. **Total: 22 points.**

### P1
A 2 names an amount or honest "I'm not sure, but here is my best estimate," a real main activity, a barrier, and a next-week change that is specific enough to try. A vague "exercise more" without a change is a 1.

### P2
Skill-related: qualities that help performance in a sport or task (agility, balance, coordination, power, speed, reaction time). Health-related: qualities tied to general health (cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition at a non-medical, general level). One fitting activity each. 1 if the examples are swapped or the definitions blur.

### P3
A four-week outline that starts from a realistic base, increases gradually (time, days, or easy/hard mix), and includes rest or an easier day. FITT language is a plus, not required. A jump from zero to daily all-out running with no rest is a 1 at most.

### P4
Patterns: midweek is the only vigorous day; Tuesday is empty; two similar walks; a short flexibility day. A consistent-moderate change might add a short walk on Tuesday, slightly lengthen a walk, or keep Friday from being a crash after Wednesday — with a reason. 1 if the pattern is noticed but the change is vague.

### P5
Two everyday habits: eat lunch, a simple snack an hour or so before practice, bring a water bottle, sip during breaks, a ordinary meal after. No supplement stacks, "cut all food after 2:00," or medical diets. 1 if only one habit is named.

### P6 — **Correct: B.**

### P7
First: stop or pause the activity; have the classmate sit out and get water or shade; tell a coach, official, or trusted adult. Winning or "not embarrassing them" is not the priority because exhaustion and confusion raise the chance of a mistake or injury. A plan that keeps them in the game is not a 2.

### P8
A respectful move: redirect to the next play, "we can talk after," one calm instruction, or asking the blamed teammate if they are okay — without piling on. A worse move: yelling back, joining the blame, or quitting in a way that only inflames the moment. Score the civic/sportsmanship reasoning, not toughness.

### P9
Coach language is descriptive ("three movement days, one rest day, one short stretch day") not shaming. The week trained endurance / moderate-to-vigorous movement most (walks + basketball). Strength training is barely present (no resistance work); flexibility appears only in a 10-minute stretch. Accept a close, justified choice.

### P10
A real recovery habit and an ordinary sign (heavy leftover fatigue, unusually poor sleep, still-exhausted legs, mood crash) plus rest or telling an adult if it persists. Do not reward medical diagnosis or medication advice. Incomplete "I never need recovery" with no sign is a 1.

### P11
Water replaces what you lose by sweating and helps you keep practicing safely in heat. A practical rule: bring a bottle, drink before and during breaks, don't wait until you feel very thirsty, or similar. No medical claims required or wanted.

### P12
A 2 considers a real risk (concrete, no pads, copycat stunts), asks at least one useful question (surface, skill, padding, an adult present), and includes a clear decline or safer-alternative sentence the student could actually say. "I'd just do it so they don't laugh" with no risk thinking is a 0 or 1 at most.
