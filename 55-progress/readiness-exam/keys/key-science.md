# Key — Science (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

**Scoring:** S1 and S11 = 1 or 0. All other items = 2 complete and scientifically accurate, 1 partial or missing a key idea, 0 blank or a scientifically false main claim. **Total: 22 points.**

### S1 — **Correct: C.** Cooking an egg changes the proteins (chemical). A, B, and D are physical changes.

### S2
Chemical: a new substance forms (often with a color change, gas not from boiling, temperature change, or irreversible change such as burning or cooking). Physical: appearance or state changes but the substance stays the same (melting, tearing, dissolving in many classroom cases). One valid example of each required.

### S3
Newton's second law (\(F = ma\)) and/or the first law (inertia). Larger mass means more force is needed for the same acceleration (harder to start) and more force is needed to decelerate it (harder to stop). A 2 uses mass and force/acceleration or inertia correctly. "The wagon is heavier so it doesn't want to move" is a 1 unless inertia/mass is named.

### S4
The skateboarder pushes backward on the ground; the ground pushes the skateboarder (and board) forward with an equal and opposite force (third law). The force pair is person-on-ground and ground-on-person (or board). 1 if "push back" is mentioned without identifying the pair.

### S5
Differences: sound is a mechanical wave and needs a medium; light is electromagnetic and can travel through vacuum (or: sound is typically longitudinal; visible light is transverse). Hearing a radio in another room: sound can diffract / travel around obstacles and through air (and walls, somewhat); light from the speaker itself is blocked by the wall so you may not see it. A 2 needs one accurate wave contrast and a plausible reason about sound traveling around or through barriers.

### S6
Luminosity and temperature let astronomers compare stars (and place them on an H-R diagram: giants, dwarfs, main sequence, etc.). The Sun is a main-sequence star of moderate temperature and luminosity — not the largest, smallest, or hottest. 1 if only "they're different" with no H-R / luminosity-temperature idea.

### S7
Two valid properties (temperature, humidity, air pressure, wind, or related: air masses, fronts, at a Grade 8 level). A change must be tied to felt weather (falling pressure often precedes a storm; high humidity makes air feel muggy; temperature drops behind a cold front).

### S8
Into the atmosphere: burning fossil fuels or wood, respiration, decomposition (CO₂ released). Taken up or stored: photosynthesis, dissolving in oceans, formation of some rocks / fossil fuels over long time. Slogans without a mechanism ("be green") are not enough for a 2.

### S9
Less energy is available at the snake than in the grass. Energy is lost at each transfer (as heat / used in life processes); only a fraction moves to the next level. 1 if "the snake gets less" with no reason.

### S10
A reasonable prediction: tan beetles are harder to see on light sand, so they are more likely to survive and reproduce; the population should, over generations, have a higher proportion of tan beetles (dark beetles become rarer). The idea is natural selection acting on variation already present — not individual beetles choosing to change color or "trying." A Lamarck-style "they become tan because they need to" is a 1 at most.

### S11 — **Correct: B.**

### S12
The claim is not sound. Mass is conserved; gas (carbon dioxide) leaves an open cup, so the remaining mixture can look "empty" or weigh less without matter being destroyed. Evidence: capture the gas in a closed (or balloon-covered) container and compare total mass before and after; it should stay essentially the same. 1 if the student says matter isn't destroyed but offers no gas / closed-container evidence.
