# Key — Reading (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

**Scoring, short constructed response (unless noted):** 2 = complete and accurate, with required evidence when asked; 1 = partially accurate or missing evidence; 0 = blank, off-prompt, or contradicted by the passage. Multiple choice: 1 if correct, 0 otherwise. Do not invent a "level" beyond this sitting's points.

**Total: 21 points** (R1, R5, R12 = 1 pt each; all others = 2 pts).

### R1 — A is wrong (too big a job). C is not in the text. D is not her stated job. **Correct: B.**

### R2
The card tells her, if a try fails, to write down the reason and then attempt the work one more time. Evidence may include the first batch breaking and Maya starting the timer again, or "High heat broke the sauce."

### R3
She almost claims a simple "yes" because the second batch looked fine, but she wants him to see the real record: it worked the *second* time and she already knows a change ("too much lemon"). Evidence: "It worked the second time... And I know what I would change" and/or showing the notebook instead of just saying yes.

### R4
She moves from almost quitting / dumping the first batch (face hot, "test was a bust") to adjusting the method, writing a precise note, and telling the truth about the second try. A strong answer names a concrete choice (turning the heat down, measuring oil more slowly, underlining the lemon note, showing the notebook).

### R5 — **Correct: C.** A overstates persuasion; B is false (informational, not a made-up family story as the main mode); D is too broad.

### R6
The fan's spinning (rotary) motion and the pump's up-and-down (reciprocating) stroke, connected by gears and the sucker rod.

### R7
Electric pumps are easier to start and stop on a schedule but fail when the power fails; a windmill can work without grid power but fails when the wind dies or the hardware breaks. Accept an equivalent contrast using passage details (power outage insurance vs. wind/hardware limits).

### R8
Here *insurance* means a backup or protection against a problem (especially a power outage), not a purchased policy. The author uses it to show that a mill that can lift water without electricity is a safeguard.

### R9
The last paragraph adds the trade-off and the idea that well-kept ranches keep more than one way to move water. Ending at paragraph three would leave the mill sounding only sturdy and useful, without the limits or the comparison to electric pumps.

### R10
The timer/notebook help Maya bound the work and record what failed and what to change; the windmill/tank (and governor) lift and store water and can stop when the tank is full. In both, checking the result matters: Maya checks the sauce and writes the lemon note; ranch care means noticing loose bolts, a weak well, or a full tank. Score 2 only if both passages are used with at least one accurate detail each.

### R11
A sound summary, in order: Maya sets a 25-minute sauce test; the first batch fails; she follows her card, tries again with lower heat and slower oil; the second batch holds but is too sharp; she writes the notes and tells her dad the truth. Deduct to 1 if events are out of order or extra invented plot is added.

### R12 — **Correct: C.** Supported by her almost saying yes, then showing the notebook. A is contradicted (she has a method and a card). B is the opposite of her final choice. D is not in the text.
