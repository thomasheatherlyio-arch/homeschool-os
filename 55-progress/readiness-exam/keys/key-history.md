# Key — History (COLT / Dad only)

Shiloh should not open this file before or during a sitting. COLT never reads a key aloud during a test.

**Scoring:** H2 and H7 = 1 or 0. All other items = 2 complete and historically accurate, 1 partial or one error, 0 blank or a major factual inversion (for example, placing Reconstruction before the Civil War as a cause). Do not deduct for a missing exact date if the sequence and ideas are right. **Total: 22 points.**

### H1
Two distinct motives (religious freedom or a faith community; profit / land / tobacco or trade; political refuge; a charter company's economic plan; etc.) plus a fitting example (Plymouth / Massachusetts and Puritans or Pilgrims; Virginia and economic opportunity; Pennsylvania and Quakers; Maryland and Catholics; Georgia and a later philanthropic / strategic aim). 1 if the colony does not match the reason.

### H2 — **Correct: C.**

### H3
Britain won but was in debt and sought more control over the colonies. Policies may include the Proclamation of 1763, new taxes (Sugar, Stamp, Townshend), tighter enforcement, or quartering. The answer should connect war debt / western land control to colonial resistance. 1 if the war is mentioned with no postwar policy.

### H4
Purpose: to announce and justify independence from Britain, and to state reasons for the break (and to rally support). Ideas: unalienable rights, consent of the governed, grievances against the king, or the right to alter or abolish an unjust government. 1 if purpose or a justifying idea is missing.

### H5
Weaknesses: no power to tax effectively, weak or no executive, difficulty regulating trade, hard to amend, no national court system of the later kind, state rivalries, inability to put down unrest easily (Shays' Rebellion is a valid illustration). Constitution: stronger federal government, Congress's power to tax, an executive, a judiciary, a better amendment process, etc. Two weaknesses + one constitutional fix required for a 2.

### H6
Valid acquisitions / changes: Louisiana Purchase (1803), Florida (Adams-Onís), Texas annexation, Oregon, Mexican Cession, Gadsden Purchase. Westward growth sharpened conflict over whether slavery would expand, Native displacement, or sectional balance in Congress. 1 if the acquisition is named but the debate is vague.

### H7 — **Correct: B.** (D is chronologically false.)

### H8
Emancipation Proclamation: shifted the war aims toward ending slavery in rebel states, discouraged foreign recognition of the Confederacy, and allowed Black enlistment in the Union army (issued as a war measure; it did not immediately free people in Union slave states). Gettysburg: major Union victory in the East that halted Lee's invasion; Vicksburg: Union control of the Mississippi and a split Confederacy (both 1863). Industry/railroads: the Union could move troops and supplies more effectively and produce more weapons and goods. A 2 needs a real detail, not only "it was important."

### H9
Goals: restore the Union; define and protect citizenship and rights of formerly enslaved people (13th–15th Amendments); rebuild the South. Changes: legal freedom, citizenship, voting rights for Black men, some officeholding, Freedmen's Bureau schools or contracts. Limits: Black Codes, violence, later Jim Crow / disenfranchisement, the end of federal troops' protection after 1877. 1 if only a goal is named, or if a setback is invented as a "success."

### H10
Order: Declaration of Independence (1776); ratification of the Constitution (1787–1788); Louisiana Purchase (1803); the Civil War (1861–1865). The sentence should show a real link, for example: independence created the need for a workable national government, which the Constitution supplied; or the Constitution created a union whose later territorial growth (Louisiana) fed sectional conflict that led to war. 1 if order is right but the linking sentence is missing or reversed.

### H11
New England: rocky soil, harbors, forests, shorter growing season — small farms, fishing, shipbuilding, trade. Middle: more mixed farming and ports (grain, commerce). South: longer growing season, fertile land — cash crops and (in time) enslaved labor on plantations, plus tidewater vs. backcountry. Two concrete details in one region required.

### H12
War years: commander of the Continental Army; holding the army together; Yorktown campaign (with allies). Presidency: precedents such as a cabinet, two-term example / Farewell Address, Neutrality, or the whiskey-tax enforcement. One accurate item from each period for a 2.
