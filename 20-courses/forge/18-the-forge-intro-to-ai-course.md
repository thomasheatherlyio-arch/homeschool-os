# Doc 18 — The Forge: Introduction to AI & Applied AI Projects Course

**Purpose:** A 10-week, project-based elective introducing Shiloh to how artificial intelligence actually works and how to use it to make real things — built the same way [[14-the-kitchen-culinary-studio|Docs 14]]–16 (The Kitchen, The Gym, The Panel) turned a real interest into a structured, self-paced curriculum that routes Texas-required and standard-practice subjects ([[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]]) through hands-on work. Where those courses each anchor in one craft (cooking, training, electrical), this one anchors in a tool that now touches almost every craft — so half the course is "how AI works" (foundations, ethics, citizenship) and half is "make something real with it" (a trained classifier, AI-generated writing/art/audio, and a capstone AI-assisted short film). Section 2 answers the specific research question this doc was built to settle: what already exists for teaching AI to kids, especially homeschoolers, and where the real gaps are. Section 9 covers Curious Refuge, the AI-filmmaking training platform Thomas asked to be included as a named curriculum option for the creative-production half of the course.

---

## 1. Why This Fits This System

- **AI literacy is becoming a baseline skill, not a niche one.** The same way [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] treats reading and math as non-negotiable, using AI tools competently, critically, and ethically is fast becoming table stakes for nearly every path on [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]]'s Horizon Ladder — college, trades, and entrepreneurship alike. [[16-the-panel-electrical-engineering-electrician-course|Doc 16]] §8 already documented how AI demand is reshaping the electrical trade from the outside; this course lets Shiloh look at the same shift from the inside, understanding the tool itself rather than only its downstream effects on other careers.
- **Every AI project is a real Venture & Craft build.** Training a classifier, writing a prompt chain, or producing a short film is a "make something and put it into the world" project, the same category [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] already treats as core Venture & Craft work — this course just adds a new toolset to that existing muscle.
- **AI ethics is a live civics lesson.** Bias in training data, deepfakes, misinformation, and academic-integrity questions around AI-assisted writing are all real, current "good citizenship" material ([[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]]) — arguably more relevant to Shiloh's actual daily life than most standard civics content, since he already lives around these tools.
- **It's a direct pipeline into [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]]'s Studio.** The capstone (Section 5, Week 10) produces an AI-assisted video — literally a piece of Studio content ([[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §2.5, Edutainment, or a new AI-Assisted pillar), so this course doesn't sit apart from the rest of the system, it feeds directly into it.
- **A finished project is real evidence.** Per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3, a working trained classifier with logged accuracy numbers, a written AI-ethics investigation, and a finished short film are a paper trail — nobody has to take "he learned about AI" on faith.

## 2. Landscape Research — What Already Exists for Teaching AI to Kids

*Researched August 2026. This section answers the question Thomas asked directly: do services or directories of project-based AI courses/videos for kids — especially homeschoolers — already exist? Short answer: yes, several, but none of them are a single, free, homeschool-branded package that covers "how AI works" *and* "produce a real creative deliverable" in one course. This doc is built as a hybrid, borrowing the strongest free pieces for foundations and layering a paid, professional-grade option (Curious Refuge, Section 9) on top for the production half.*

### 2.1 Free, Structured Curricula (foundations-focused)

| Resource | What it is | Ages | Project-based? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Code.org — AI Discoveries / AI for Oceans** | Free, self-paced online modules teaching ML basics through guided activities | ~10–18 | Light — guided exercises more than open builds | Widely used in schools; good, safe on-ramp; not deep |
| **Day of AI (MIT-affiliated)** | Free, ready-to-teach units spanning Pre-K through high school, including a project-based "Foundations of CS and AI Systems" track for grades 7–12 | 4–18 | Yes, especially the 7–12 track | Broadest free option found; includes ethics and cross-subject (ELA, social studies, science) units; a "Student Voices in AI" youth council exists for older teens |
| **AI4K12 — Resource Directory** | A curated *directory* (not a course itself) of AI teaching tools, robots, videos, and curricula, maintained by the national AI4K12 initiative | K–12 | N/A (it's the directory) | This is the closest thing to "a directory of AI learning resources for kids" that Thomas asked about — worth bookmarking as a standing reference, not a one-time read |
| **Machine Learning for Kids** | Free, browser-based tool pairing with Scratch to let kids train their own text/image/number classifiers, then use them inside a Scratch game | ~8–16 | Yes — the whole point is building a working project | Strong fit for Week 3 below |
| **Google Teachable Machine** | Free, browser-based tool for training image/sound/pose classifiers with no code | 8+ | Yes | Fastest, lowest-friction "train a real model" experience available; used in Week 2 |
| **Common Sense Education — AI Literacy Lessons (Grades 6–12)** | Free lesson collection focused on ethics, bias, and responsible use rather than technical building | 11–18 | Mixed | Best free source found specifically for the ethics/citizenship week |
| **TeachAI.org Toolkit** | Guidance and resource toolkit aimed at schools adopting AI, cross-referenced heavily by AI4K12 | School-facing | N/A | Useful for Thomas as the "administrator" reviewing what schools are being told, less directly useful to Shiloh |

### 2.2 Paid / Live Options

| Resource | Format | Ages | Approx. cost (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Outschool — AI Explorers and related classes** | Live, small-group online classes | 10–14+ | Per-class pricing, roughly $10–30/session | Broad marketplace with many individual teachers/classes rather than one fixed curriculum; quality varies by teacher |
| **Create & Learn** | Live, small-group | 7–18 | ~$15–30/session | Similar marketplace model to Outschool |
| **Inspirit AI** | Live, more intensive | 13–18 | $400–800 | More rigorous/college-prep-flavored; not homeschool-specific |
| **LittleAIMaster** | Self-paced app, 540+ short chapters | Grades 6–12 | ~$11.67/mo ($139.99/yr) | Concept-first, works offline; not project-heavy |
| **FundaFunda Academy — "Artificial Intelligence for Teens"** | Online course, weekly research assignments + group discussion, explicitly reviewed by a homeschool blog | Teen | Not published | The one option found that's explicitly homeschool-market-facing; covers what AI is, tool exposure (ChatGPT, Canva AI, Perplexity), red-teaming exercises, and a capstone (one reviewed student built a full AI-generated music album — design, lyrics, melody). Worth knowing about as a comparison point even though this doc builds Shiloh's own version instead of buying it. |

### 2.3 Curated Video "Directories" for Kids

Thomas specifically asked about existing video directories. The closest matches:

- **Crash Course: Artificial Intelligence** (PBS/Crash Course, 20 episodes) — the single most-recommended general AI video series across every source checked; appropriate for a bright 14-year-old.
- **TED-Ed AI/tech playlists** — short, animated, well-produced; skews toward older kids/teens.
- **"Neural Networks for Babies"-style simplified explainers** and **freeCodeCamp's kids-friendly AI/coding playlist** — good slower-paced supplements.
- **startaiway.com's "AI Education Content for Kids" post** functions as an informal curated list itself (channels like STEMpedia, Simplilearn's "AI for Kids," Smart Learning for Kids), but it's a blog post, not a maintained service — nothing found rises to the level of an actively-curated, ongoing "video directory for homeschool AI education" the way [[08-mind-mansion-living-library|Doc 08]]'s Mansion curates books.

### 2.4 The Verdict — Why This Doc Builds a Hybrid Instead of Adopting One Existing Option

No single free or paid option does everything this course wants: (1) real "how AI works" foundations, (2) a real trained model students can point to, (3) honest ethics/citizenship content, and (4) a genuine creative production pipeline ending in a shareable deliverable. Day of AI comes closest on the free-and-project-based side but stops short of professional-grade creative production; Curious Refuge is the strongest option found for the creative-production side but assumes an adult/professional audience and isn't built to teach AI fundamentals at all. So this course does what [[14-the-kitchen-culinary-studio|Doc 14]]–16 already did with cooking, fitness, and electrical work: borrow the best free structured pieces for the foundational weeks (Section 5, Weeks 1–7), then bring in a real, named, professional-grade platform — Curious Refuge — for the production half (Weeks 8–10), the same way those courses eventually point toward a real certification or licensing body rather than staying purely homemade.

## 5. Course Structure — 10 Weeks

Suggested pace: **one unit per week**, running as an afternoon/dedicated block. Weeks build on each other, but Shiloh can move faster through anything already comfortable (Section 6).

| Week | Focus | Key skills | Deliverable / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. What Is AI, Really?** | Rule-based programs vs. machine learning, narrow vs. general AI, a short history of the field (symbolic AI → ML → generative AI boom) | Distinguishing real AI capability from hype, basic vocabulary | A one-page "what AI actually is" summary, in Shiloh's own words |
| **2. Teaching a Computer to See** | How a model learns from labeled examples; training, testing, and accuracy | Using Google Teachable Machine to train an image or sound classifier | A working Teachable Machine classifier + a logged accuracy number and one paragraph on what confused it |
| **3. Building With What You Trained** | Turning a trained model into an actual mini-project via Machine Learning for Kids + Scratch | Combining a trained classifier into a working small game or tool | A finished Scratch project that uses a Shiloh-trained model |
| **4. Generative AI — Text** | How large language models predict text; prompting as a real skill; where they're useful vs. where they're wrong | Writing effective prompts, fact-checking AI output, revising AI-assisted drafts | A short piece of writing co-created with a chatbot, with a reflection noting exactly what the AI got right and wrong |
| **5. Generative AI — Images & Audio** | How image/audio generation tools work at a conceptual level; prompt engineering for visual/audio media | Iterating on prompts, curating outputs, basic style direction | An AI-illustrated short story, comic, or album-style cover, plus one narrated audio clip |
| **6. AI Ethics, Bias & Citizenship** | Bias in training data, deepfakes and misinformation, data privacy, why "AI said so" isn't proof of anything, academic-integrity norms | Investigating a real example of AI bias or a viral AI-misinformation case | A short bias-audit write-up + a one-page personal "AI use policy" Shiloh writes and signs for himself |
| **7. AI & the Future of Work** | How AI is reshaping careers (including the trades — see [[16-the-panel-electrical-engineering-electrician-course|Doc 16]] §8 for the electrician side of this same story), and what new AI-adjacent careers actually look like (prompt engineer, ML engineer, AI content creator/producer) | Research, comparing career paths, running a real outreach contact | A research report or a logged Doc 10 Rung 1 informational interview with someone who uses AI professionally |
| **8. Learning to Produce — Curious Refuge Fundamentals** | The free Curious Refuge "Start Here" course: AI storytelling concepts, filmmaking/advertising workflows, the current AI-tool landscape | Understanding a real, professional AI-production pipeline | Notes from the free course + a one-page plan for what Shiloh wants his own short film to be about |
| **9. Produce a Short AI-Assisted Film** | Applying Week 8's concepts hands-on: AI-assisted scriptwriting, AI-generated visuals, AI voice/sound, basic editing | Running an actual small production pipeline start to finish | A rough-cut short film (1–3 minutes) |
| **10. Capstone — Finish, Present, Reflect** | Polishing the Week 9 film (or, if Shiloh prefers, a different capstone — see Section 12) and presenting it | Finishing under a deadline, presenting work, honest self-assessment | See Section 12 |

## 6. Skill Placement — Start Where He Actually Is

Run a quick, honest self-check with Shiloh before Week 1 locks in (COLT can run this conversationally), same approach as [[14-the-kitchen-culinary-studio|Docs 14]]–16:

- **Already comfortable using chatbots/AI tools casually** — Weeks 1 and 4 can compress into a review rather than new material; spend the extra time going deeper on Weeks 2–3's hands-on model training, which is the part most casual AI users never actually do.
- **New to AI tools generally, comfortable with tech otherwise** — run the full 10 weeks at full length; this is genuinely new ground and worth the complete pace.
- **Already interested in filmmaking/video (per [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]]'s Studio work)** — Weeks 8–10 are likely the highest-pull stretch of the course; consider letting that interest pull the whole course forward, front-loading extra time there rather than rushing past Weeks 1–3's fundamentals to get there faster.

**One rule that doesn't flex regardless of skill level:** every AI tool account this course uses runs through Thomas's account and supervision, not a standalone account created for Shiloh — see Section 10 for why.

## 9. Deep Dive — Curious Refuge as a Curriculum Option

*Researched August 2026. Pricing and course lineup can shift — reverify directly on curiousrefuge.com before committing money.*

**What it is:** Curious Refuge is a professional training platform built for artists, filmmakers, and advertisers learning to use generative AI tools in real production work — script-to-screen training used by working studio artists, not a kids' education company. It's being named here specifically because it's the strongest real, professional-grade option found anywhere in this research for the *creative-production* half of this course — nothing built for kids goes nearly this deep on actually producing a finished piece of AI media.

**The free tier — the actual recommended starting point (Week 8):** Curious Refuge's free "Start Here" course covers AI storytelling concepts, AI filmmaking and advertising workflows, a curated list of current AI tools, access to a members-only community chat, and weekly AI news updates. This is genuinely useful and costs nothing — Week 8 above is built directly around it.

**The paid catalog, if Weeks 8–9 confirm real interest:**

| Option | Price (2026) | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Single course (e.g., AI Filmmaking, AI for YouTubers) | $749 | 4 weeks of video modules, weekly homework with critique, downloadable templates, community access |
| All-Access membership | $149/month | Every course in the catalog, career services, pro critique, live Q&A with industry experts |
| 3-course bundle | $1,799 | Three courses, 12 months of content updates, all the single-course features |
| 5-course bundle | $2,699 | Five courses, same terms as above |

**The honest read on cost vs. fit:** this is real money, priced for working professionals, not a kids' add-on purchase. The free tier plus Weeks 8–9's hands-on practice using free/low-cost AI tools (whatever image, video, and voice tools Thomas and Shiloh choose together at the time) is enough to run the full course as written. A paid single course is worth treating as a genuine stretch investment — the same category as [[15-the-gym-personal-training-fitness-course|Doc 15]]'s $999+ personal-training certifications or [[16-the-panel-electrical-engineering-electrician-course|Doc 16]]'s licensing fees — to be decided by Thomas specifically, only after the small bet (Section 8) confirms this is a real, sustained interest rather than a one-course curiosity.

**Age/account note:** Curious Refuge's site states no explicit age requirement or minor policy either way. Given that and the professional framing of the content, any paid enrollment should run through Thomas's account with Thomas reviewing course material alongside Shiloh, the same access-and-supervision pattern [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §8 already established for NotebookLM — Shiloh directs the work and does the actual producing; the account and the purchase decision are Thomas's.

**What this doesn't replace:** Curious Refuge assumes the learner already wants to make AI-assisted films — it doesn't teach what a neural network is or address AI ethics at all. That's exactly why it sits at Week 8 of this course rather than Week 1: the foundations (Weeks 1–7) have to come first for this to be a real "understand it, then use it" course rather than "use it without understanding it."

## 10. AI Tool Accounts & Safety Notes

Nearly every major AI tool (ChatGPT, image generators, Discord-based communities many of these tools use) sets a minimum age — commonly 13, with parental consent required under 18 in most platforms' terms of service. Rather than creating standalone youth accounts tool-by-tool, this course follows the same pattern already established in [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §8 for NotebookLM and reaffirmed in Section 9 above:

- **All AI tool accounts used in this course run through Thomas's account**, with Shiloh directing the actual work — writing the prompts, making the creative decisions, doing the reviewing and revising. A minor's real authorship doesn't require a minor's account.
- **No personal, identifying, or location information** goes into any prompt, ever — treat every AI tool the same way [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §6 treats anything that could eventually go public.
- **The Week 6 ethics unit comes before any heavy creative production (Weeks 8–10)** on purpose — talking honestly about deepfakes and likeness misuse matters more, not less, once Shiloh is actually capable of producing convincing AI media.
- **The Section 12 capstone follows [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §6's publishing default:** start private, Thomas signs off before anything goes public, same as every other Studio piece.

## 12. Capstone — Finish, Present, Reflect

**Required minimum:** Shiloh finishes the Week 9 rough-cut short film to a genuinely completed state — edited, paced, with titles/credits — and presents it to Thomas, walking through the actual production pipeline he used (script/idea → AI-generated visuals → voice/audio → edit) and naming, honestly, which parts AI actually helped with and which parts still needed his own judgment and work.

**Alternate capstone (Shiloh's choice, in place of the film):** if the technical/building side of the course (Weeks 2–3) pulled harder than the creative-production side, Shiloh can instead extend and polish his Week 3 trained-model project into something more ambitious — a more accurate classifier, a more complete Scratch game built around it — and present that instead, with the same "walk through the pipeline and be honest about what worked" standard.

**Either version:**
- Gets logged in the Evidence Inbox ([[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] §2.3) with the finished piece and a short written reflection.
- Closes with an honest Interest Ledger entry ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §6) — confirmed / ruled out / still unclear — on whether AI (technical side, creative side, both, or neither) is worth a longer-horizon look.
- Follows the [[11-the-studio-experiential-learning-menu|Doc 11]] §6 privacy default if Shiloh wants it to go beyond family viewing.
