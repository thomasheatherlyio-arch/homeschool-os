## 5. Skill Placement — Start Where He Actually Is

Starting point, as Shiloh described it on 2026-11-22 (confirm again before Unit 0 locks in; COLT can run this conversationally):

- **Has played *Civilization VI* once.** He knows the basic mechanics (founding a city, moving units, opening the tech/civics trees) but **not strategy and not the victory conditions**.
- **Got bored and just waged war.** That is useful data, not a problem to scold. War is a real part of history and stays in the course as a thing to study (costs, warmongering, loyalty, grievances). It is not the only way to play, and it is not the win condition for Campaign 1.
- **Therefore Unit 0 is not optional and is not skipped.** The first guided game uses a **non-domination** victory path he chooses after Unit 0. He may still fight wars; he has to log why, and what they cost.

Flex after Unit 0, same approach as [[kitchen-course|Docs 14]]–16:

- **Victory path feels clear, early strategy is starting to click** — keep Campaign 1 going and use weekly units mainly for reading, Game vs. Reality, and outputs; don't restart unless a unit truly needs a short second game as a named civ.
- **Still defaulting to "declare war on everyone" when bored** — shorten the play block, fill the Decision Log *during* or immediately after the session (not the next day), and let COLT ask one "what else could you have built instead?" question before the next session. Do not ban war.
- **Already strong on a stretch of real history** (for example, he already knows the U.S. founding well) — compress that week's reading into a review and spend the extra time on the Game vs. Reality critique and the civic-action work.

**One rule that doesn't flex:** a play session without a Decision Log entry does not count as course work, regardless of skill level.
