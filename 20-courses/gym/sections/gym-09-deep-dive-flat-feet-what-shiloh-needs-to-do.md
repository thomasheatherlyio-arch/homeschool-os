## 9. Deep Dive — Flat Feet: What Shiloh Needs to Do

*Researched August 2026, general/educational information — not a medical diagnosis. If Shiloh hasn't had his feet formally evaluated by a podiatrist or physical therapist, that's worth doing before locking in a long-term corrective program, since severity and cause change what actually helps. This section is a research-informed starting point, and a genuinely good Unit 7 assessment case study — Shiloh is the subject and the researcher at once.*

### 9.1 What Flat Feet (Pes Planus) Actually Is

Flat feet means the medial longitudinal arch — the curve that normally runs along the inside of the foot — is collapsed or absent, so the sole sits closer to flat against the ground. Common causes include a weakened posterior tibial tendon (the most common acquired cause), underdeveloped intrinsic foot muscles, genetic ligament laxity, tight calves/Achilles tendons, prolonged wear of overly cushioned shoes, extra body weight, or a past ankle/foot injury.

### 9.2 Why It Matters Beyond the Foot Itself

The arch's real job is to act as a flexible shock absorber during walking, running, and jumping. When it collapses, the effects don't stay local — they travel up the whole kinetic chain:

- **Ankle:** overpronation (the foot rolls inward) and reduced ankle dorsiflexion, which limits how deep Shiloh can safely squat and changes how force moves through the ankle on every step or jump.
- **Knee:** the lower leg's internal rotation pulls the knees inward (valgus collapse) during squats, jumps, and cuts — exactly the kind of movement basketball demands constantly, and a real risk factor for knee injury over time.
- **Hip:** the same internal-rotation chain reaches the hip, contributing to poor hip mechanics and postural compensation.
- **Lower back:** the cumulative rotation up the chain can drive lower-crossed-syndrome-type posture and chronic lower back pain.

In plain terms: flat feet aren't just a foot issue for an athlete — they can quietly shape how the knees, hips, and low back move on every single step, jump, and landing.

### 9.3 The Corrective Plan

Conservative, exercise-based correction is the standard first-line approach (versus jumping straight to orthotics or surgery, which are reserved for pain that doesn't respond to exercise, or more severe/rigid cases). A phased plan, with real improvement typically showing up over **3–6 months of consistent work** — not days or weeks:

**Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4) — Wake the arch up:**
- *Short foot exercise* — standing or seated, try to "dome" the arch by pulling the ball of the foot toward the heel without curling the toes. 10 reps, hold 5 seconds, both feet.
- *Towel scrunches* — scrunch a towel toward you using only the toes. 2–3 sets of 10.
- *Toe yoga* — lift just the big toe while keeping the other four flat, then reverse; builds independent toe control. 10 reps each foot.
- *Tibialis posterior activation* — resisted foot inversion with a light band. 2–3 sets of 12–15.

**Phase 2 (Weeks 3–8) — Build real strength, overlapping with Phase 1:**
- *Single-leg calf raises, focused on the arch* — rise slowly, keeping weight through the big toe/second toe, not rolling to the outside edge. 3 sets of 10–12 per foot.
- *Heel walking* — walk 20–30 feet on heels only, toes lifted.
- *Marble/towel pickups* — pick up small objects with the toes, one at a time.
- *Single-leg balance with active arch engagement* — 3 rounds of 20–30 seconds per foot, doming the arch throughout.

**Phase 3 (Ongoing):**
- *Ankle dorsiflexion mobilization* (knee-to-wall stretch) — directly counteracts the tight-calf contributor to flat feet.
- *Plantar fascia release* — rolling the arch over a ball for 1–2 minutes per foot.
- *Barefoot training integration* — light, controlled barefoot work (on safe surfaces) to keep building intrinsic foot strength once the base work above is solid.

This whole protocol slots directly into the stretching routine below (Section 10) and the ongoing training log (Section 7) — it doesn't need to be a separate project, just a consistent 5–10 minutes worked into what Shiloh's already doing.
