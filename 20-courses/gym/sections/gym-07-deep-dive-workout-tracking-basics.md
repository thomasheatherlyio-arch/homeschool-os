## 7. Deep Dive — Workout Tracking Basics

Tracking is what turns "I worked out" into real, comparable evidence — the same "evidence over assertion" logic ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3) that runs through this whole system.

**What to track, every session:**

| Metric | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight/load** | How much resistance was used | The core variable for strength progress |
| **Reps** | How many repetitions per set | Combined with load, shows total effort |
| **Sets** | How many rounds of that rep count | Part of total volume |
| **Volume load** | Weight × reps × sets, added up | The single best "am I actually progressing" number — e.g., 3 sets of 10 at 100 lbs = 3,000 lbs of volume that session |
| **RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)** | A 1–10 self-rated effort scale | Tracks intensity even when weight isn't changing week to week |
| **Rest time** | Time between sets | Affects which energy system is being trained (Section 6.1) |

**Progressive overload** — the core principle behind all real progress — just means gradually increasing training difficulty over time (more weight, more reps, more sets, less rest, or better form at the same load) so the body keeps being forced to adapt instead of plateauing. A simple beginner rule: work up to the top of a rep range (e.g., 3×10) with good form, then add a small amount of weight and drop back to the bottom of the range, and repeat.

**Logging methods** — any of these work; the point is consistency, not the tool:

| Method | Good for |
|---|---|
| Physical notebook | Simplicity, no screens, satisfying to fill in |
| Spreadsheet | Easy to build formulas for volume load and track long-term trends |
| Note-taking app | Flexible, fast, always in his pocket |
| Dedicated workout-tracking app | Built-in graphs and automatic progress comparisons |

**Common mistakes to avoid:** logging inconsistently (a log with gaps can't show real trends), chasing heavier weight while letting form break down, and changing exercises too often to ever see whether progress is actually happening.

A working log template (ready to copy) is in Section 11.
