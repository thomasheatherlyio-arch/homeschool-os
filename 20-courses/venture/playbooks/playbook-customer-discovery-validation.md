# Playbook 2: Customer Discovery & Validation

Linked to: [[playbooks-index]] | [[playbook-idea-research-scorecard]] | [[shiloh-playbook]]

## Purpose
Confirm real US homeschool parents actually feel the problem and will take action (email signup or pre-order) *before* spending weeks polishing code.

## When to Use
- Immediately after an idea scores >=60 in [[playbook-idea-research-scorecard]].
- Whenever conversion stalls and you need to know *why* buyers aren't buying.

## Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Safety & Dad Approval First**: Shiloh never cold-DMs strangers privately. Thomas vets and invites 5–10 homeschool parents (co-ops, friends, X/LinkedIn connections) and joins every live call.
2. **Run 5–10 "Mom Test" Interviews (15–20 mins each)**:
   - Rule: **Never pitch your product in the first 12 minutes.** Ask about their *past behavior* and *current pain*, not hypotheticals ("Would you buy X?" is banned!).
3. **Extract Exact Quotes**: Log the exact words parents use to describe their frustration—those become your landing page headline.
4. **Design a <$25 Weekend Smoke Test**:
   - Build a simple 1-page preview (free Carrd/GitHub Pages/HTML + <$15 domain) showing 2 screenshots of the sanitized dashboard and 1 clear CTA: *"Get Early Access ($29 Founding Price — Notify Me / Reserve)"*.
   - Budget: **$0–$25 max** (domain or tiny $10–$20 targeted test if Dad approves).
5. **Measure Real Numbers (7 days)**:
   - Pass bar: **>= 15% of visitors leave an email** OR **>= 3 strangers click "Reserve at $29" / reply wanting beta access**.
   - Fail bar: < 5% interest -> Do NOT build the full product yet; interview why and tweak the angle.

## Parent Interview Script (Mom Test Style)
*Intro (Dad opens, Shiloh leads questions): "Hi! We're doing research for a homeschool project—nothing to sell today. We'd love 15 minutes to learn how you manage your teen's daily school workflow."*
1. *"Walk me through yesterday morning—how did your teen know what to work on?"*
2. *"Where do things usually break down or cause nagging during the week?"*
3. *"What tools have you tried in the last year (planners, Notion, apps, AI), and what happened to them?"*
4. *"How much time do you spend each week checking work or building lesson/schedule plans?"*
5. *"Have you paid for any templates, planners, or AI tools for homeschooling? What did they cost, and were they worth it?"*
6. *(Last 3 mins only)* *"We built a private setup where an AI coach checks in with the student in Obsidian and reports to the parent. What sounds helpful—or terrifying/annoying—about that?"*
7. *"Can I send you a 2-minute demo link in two weeks if we turn this into a starter kit?"*
