## 11. Capstone — Build, Test, Pitch

**Required minimum, two parts:**

1. **The build:** Shiloh designs, builds, and tests a real (low-voltage, unenergized-current) circuit project on a trainer kit or breadboard — something with a switch, at least one series and one parallel section, and a measurable output (an LED, a small motor, a buzzer). He documents it with photos, a labeled diagram, and multimeter readings, walking Thomas through how it works using Section 3's vocabulary (voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's Law).
2. **The pitch:** a short, real business-plan pitch — "[Shiloh's Name] Electric" — using Section 9's licensing ladder and Section 10's real income numbers to lay out a realistic path from apprentice to owning a company with employees. This isn't a fantasy pitch deck; it should use the actual TDLR hour requirements and actual income ranges researched in this course, the same "use real numbers" standard [[kitchen-course|Doc 14]]'s costing exercises use.

**Stretch goal (optional):** if Week 11's informational interview or job shadow happened, fold a short clip or a direct quote from that real electrician into the pitch — grounding the plan in something Shiloh actually heard from someone doing the work, not just research.

Either version gets filmed if Shiloh wants it to double as Studio content (Section 13).
