## 1. Why This Fits This System

Electrical work is unusual among the trades on Doc 10's menu because almost none of it can be learned by hand-skill alone — it's built on real math and real physics, which makes it an unusually strong vehicle for exactly the kind of interest-routed academics [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] §6 calls for:

- **Every circuit is an algebra problem.** Ohm's Law (`V = I × R`) and Watt's Law (`P = V × I`) aren't abstract formulas here — they're the actual tool an electrician uses to size a wire or figure out why a breaker keeps tripping. Doc 09's existing GED-readiness math ("algebra-readiness math," "reasoning through language arts") gets a direct, motivated home.
- **Every panel is a systems-thinking exercise.** A house's electrical system is one interconnected system — service entrance, main panel, branch circuits, grounding — with safety margins built in on purpose (breaker sizing, wire ampacity). That's engineering thinking, not just trade knowledge, and it's the literal "basics of engineering" thread Thomas asked this course to carry.
- **Every code requirement is a civics lesson in disguise.** The National Electrical Code (NEC) exists because ungrounded, overloaded, or badly-installed wiring burns houses down and kills people — licensing (Section 9) exists to protect the public, not to gatekeep a trade. That's [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]]'s "good citizenship" subject, taught through something real instead of a textbook chapter.
- **The career ladder is unusually visible and well-documented.** Unlike a lot of career paths at 14, "apprentice → journeyman → master → business owner" is a real, numbered, publicly-tracked ladder in Texas (Section 9) — hours logged, licenses earned, income data by stage (Section 10). That kind of visible structure is exactly what [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]]'s Horizon Ladder needs to work with.
- **A finished, tested project is real evidence** — per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3, a working circuit that Shiloh built and tested with a meter, and a real business-plan pitch backed by real numbers, are a paper trail nobody has to take on faith.

This also slots directly into Doc 10's existing Trade Menu (§3) — electrical is already listed there as a candidate, tied to the South Texas Electrical JATC and TDLR licensing. This course is what turns that one table row into a real, tested 3-month exposure.
