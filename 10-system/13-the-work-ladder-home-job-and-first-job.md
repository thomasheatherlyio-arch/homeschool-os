# Doc 13 — The Work Ladder: Home Job & First Job (HTeaO)

**Purpose:** [[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]] doesn't have a standalone "Work" quadrant — work lives across **Venture & Craft** (building/earning) and **Systems & Self-Command** (follow-through, reliability). This doc is where those two quadrants meet in the most literal way possible: Shiloh's actual job history, starting today. It operationalizes two rungs:

- **Rung 0 — Home Job.** Household contribution, treated as a real job with real duties, real standards, and real accountability — not "helping out sometimes."
- **Rung 1 — First Paid Job (HTeaO).** External paid employment, using the Home Job as the track record that earns it.

This is Doc 10's **Rung 4 (Limited Paid Work)** made concrete and specific to Shiloh, and it's the most direct, literal expression of [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 5 ("contracts are literal, not metaphorical") this system has outside the Founding Charter itself.

---

## 1. The Logic: Why Home Comes First

A first paid job runs on exactly three things: **showing up when you said you would, doing the task to a real standard without being reminded twice, and being honest when something goes wrong.** None of that requires a workplace to learn — it requires a job. The Home Job is that job. It exists so that by the time Shiloh sits down for an interview at HTeaO, "reliable" isn't a claim he's making about himself — it's a track record Thomas can actually vouch for, with weeks of evidence behind it ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3).

This also mirrors how Doc 10 already treats trade exposure: nobody walks into a two-to-four-year apprenticeship without testing the work first. A first job is the same kind of commitment at a smaller scale — worth building the muscle on low-stakes ground (home) before the stakes are real (an employer who can just let him go).

## 2. Rung 0 — The Home Job

### 2.1 Job Description

Treat this section as a real, literal job posting — write it with Shiloh, not for him, the same way every other choice in this system gets made with him rather than handed down ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 2).

| Field | Fill in together |
|---|---|
| **Job title** | e.g. "Household Operations" — pick something Shiloh would actually put on a resume line, half-seriously |
| **Core duties (baseline, unpaid)** | The non-negotiable list — room upkeep, trash, dishes, laundry, pet care, yard work, whatever actually applies at home |
| **Standard of "done"** | For each duty, what "done right" looks like, not just "done" — a made bed vs. a bed with blankets thrown on it. Vague standards are the #1 reason chore systems fail; write them down once and stop relitigating them weekly |
| **Schedule** | Daily duties vs. weekly duties, and when they're due (e.g., "kitchen after dinner nightly," "trash out Sunday night") |
| **Extras menu (paid, see 2.2)** | Bigger, optional tasks with a dollar value attached |

### 2.2 Pay Structure — Baseline Unpaid, Extras Paid

Core household contribution is unpaid, framed honestly: being part of a household comes with responsibilities, the same way being part of a family isn't a transaction. This is deliberate — it's the "you don't get paid to be a decent member of an institution" lesson, and it's a real-world truth (nobody pays you extra for showing up to your own life prepared).

**Extras are where pay lives.** Above-and-beyond tasks — deep-cleaning a space, a yard project, helping with something bigger than the daily baseline — get a set price, agreed *before* the work starts, paid on completion and verified. This models the actual structure of freelance/contract work: scope it, price it, deliver it, get paid. Build the Extras Menu together and revisit it as needs change:

| Extra task | Agreed price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *(example)* Deep-clean garage | $20 | One-time, scope confirmed before starting |
| *(example)* Wash + vacuum car | $10 | Weekly option, standing offer |

### 2.3 Accountability System

Real jobs have real consequences for missed work, but the *first* response is always diagnosis, not punishment ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 1 — Shiloh's original struggle was a systems gap, not a character gap, and that lens applies here too). This system layers both, deliberately:

1. **First miss (any given week):** A direct conversation — what happened, was it a one-off, was it a system problem (forgot, no reminder, task was unclear, genuinely too much that day)? Fix the system if the system's the problem.
2. **Repeat miss (same duty, same week, or a pattern across weeks):** A real consequence attaches — docked pay from that week's Extras (if any were earned) or a tied privilege (screen time, an outing, whatever's currently meaningful) — *and* the diagnosis conversation still happens. The consequence and the conversation aren't either/or; a real job would do both (a write-up *and* a conversation about what went wrong).
3. **Chronic pattern (flagged at the Friday Review, [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]):** Gets raised explicitly at the next Quarterly Headmaster Review ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §5) as its own agenda item — not to escalate punishment further, but because a chronic miss is exactly the kind of signal [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 1 says deserves a real system redesign, not just repeating the same consequence and hoping it lands differently.

This graduated shape is also honest preparation for HTeaO: real employers document a pattern before they let someone go. Shiloh should learn what that documentation-then-consequence rhythm feels like somewhere the stakes are recoverable.

### 2.4 What the Home Job Is Actually Teaching

Worth naming out loud so it doesn't just feel like chores — this is the same skill set HTeaO (or any employer) will actually be evaluating:

- **Reliability** — doing the thing on the schedule, without being asked twice. This is [[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]'s Systems & Self-Command quadrant, applied to the most concrete possible testing ground.
- **Standards** — the difference between "technically done" and "done right," and taking pride in the difference.
- **Initiative** — noticing something needs doing before being told (the dishwasher's full, the trash is about to overflow) — the trait every "extras" job actually rewards.
- **Receiving feedback without collapsing or arguing** — a Friday Review correction is low-stakes practice for a manager's correction later.
- **Ownership of mistakes** — reporting a broken thing or a missed task honestly and early, not hiding it — directly feeds [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]]'s "communicate blockers before deadlines rather than after" clause.

### 2.5 Home Job Contract

A real, signed one-pager, modeled on the Founding Agreement ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §7) — same literal-contracts principle, scaled down:

> **Home Job Agreement**
>
> **Parties:** Thomas Heatherly ("Employer/Headmaster") and Shiloh Heatherly ("Employee/Student-CEO")
>
> **Term:** Effective the date signed, reviewed each Quarterly Headmaster Review, renewable/amendable at that checkpoint.
>
> **Employee's obligations:** Complete the core duties listed in §2.1 on the stated schedule, to the stated standard, without requiring more than one reminder. Communicate honestly and early if a duty will be missed. Treat Extras Menu tasks (§2.2) as real commitments once agreed to.
>
> **Employer's obligations:** Keep the duties list and standards current and clearly written. Pay agreed Extras prices promptly on verified completion. Give direct, specific feedback at the Friday Review rather than silent frustration. Treat the Home Job as real evidence when it's time to support a HTeaO application or reference.
>
> **Accountability:** Per §2.3 above.
>
> **Review:** Weekly at the Friday Review ([[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]); formally at each Quarterly Headmaster Review.
>

## 3. Rung 1 — First Paid Job: HTeaO

### 3.1 Fit Check

HTeaO is a walk-up/drive-through tea and beverage counter — that puts it squarely in the **food service** category Texas names as open to 14–15-year-olds (Doc 10 §2), which means age isn't the blocker here; readiness and hours are. The actual work is a good match for a first job generally: customer-facing service, following a repeatable prep process, cash/POS handling, and keeping a clean, fast-moving station — all learnable, all things the Home Job already starts building.

### 3.2 Texas Hour Rules (reconfirm before scheduling anything — Doc 10 §2 for full detail)

| Rule | Limit at 14–15 |
|---|---|
| Daily/weekly hours | Max 8 hrs/day, 48 hrs/week (Texas cap) |
| School-day hours | Max 3 hrs on a school day, 18 hrs in a school week (federal, tighter than the TX cap and controls) |
| Time-of-day | 7 a.m.–7 p.m. during the school year (7 a.m.–9 p.m. in summer); no work before 5 a.m. or past 10 p.m. on a school night under TX rules |

Reverify these directly with HTeaO's hiring contact when it's time to actually schedule — hour rules and any location-specific policy can shift, and Doc 10 already flags this as worth a live check rather than assuming.

### 3.3 Using the Existing Contact

Since there's already a contact at HTeaO, the move is to activate the **Exposure Ladder** (Doc 10 §1) with him deliberately rather than jumping straight to "can I have a job":

1. **Start with an informational conversation, even with an existing contact** — ask what the actual day-to-day looks like, what they look for in a first-time 14–15-year-old hire, and what their process is (application, interview, trial shift, paperwork). This costs nothing and turns a vague "in" into real information.
2. **Ask directly what they'd want to see before hiring a 14-year-old** — this becomes the literal target list for §3.4 below, replacing any guessing with their actual answer.
3. **Log the conversation** in the Work Log (§4) the same day, per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3 (evidence over assertion) and the same discipline Doc 10 uses for its Exposure Log.
4. **Thomas's sign-off is still required** before any actual shift, trial, or formal application moves forward — real-world logistics and safety are a Headmaster call ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §4), the same rule Doc 10 applies to job shadows.

### 3.4 Readiness Checklist — What "Ready to Apply" Looks Like

Rather than a fixed timeline, readiness is evidence-based, pulled straight from the Home Job Log (§4):

- A run of consecutive weeks (start with a target of **4 straight weeks**, adjust once real data comes in) with the Home Job fully met — no repeat misses.
- At least one Extras task completed to full standard with no rework needed.
- Comfortable talking through §2.3-style feedback without getting defensive — ask this honestly at a Friday Review before treating it as met.
- A simple one-page "resume" drafted (see §3.5) — the *act* of writing it is itself useful, even for a first job.

Once this checklist is met, that's the trigger to go back to the HTeaO contact and move from "informational conversation" toward an actual application/interview.

### 3.5 A First "Resume" (Worth Doing Even at 14)

A one-pager, built together: name/contact, a short "About" line, "Experience" (the Home Job counts — list it like a real job: title, duties, how long, what he's responsible for), basketball (discipline, teamwork), any Venture & Craft small bets ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]]) that show initiative, and availability (school schedule, hour limits from §3.2). This is also good practice for the same evidence-over-assertion habit the rest of this system builds — the resume only works if it's true and specific.

### 3.6 Once Hired — What Changes

- [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]'s Friday Review picks up HTeaO shifts the same way it tracks any other quadrant evidence — hours worked, what went well/poorly, any feedback from a manager.
- The Home Job doesn't go away — it's still the baseline, and it's still useful precedent when the two ever conflict (e.g., a missed home duty on a day he worked a shift is a scheduling problem to solve, not an excuse).
- Real pay opens a natural, separate financial-literacy thread (budgeting, saving, a spend/save/give split) — worth a short Compass conversation when it happens, but intentionally out of scope for this doc so it doesn't dilute the accountability framework above.

## 5. Cadence

- **Weekly:** Home Job reviewed at the Friday Review ([[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]) alongside the rest of the week — same evidence, same honesty standard as every other quadrant.
- **As-needed:** Any HTeaO contact, conversation, or step gets logged the same day (§4.2) and needs Thomas's sign-off before it becomes a real-world commitment (§3.3).
- **Quarterly:** Home Job pattern (chronic misses, if any) and HTeaO progress both get a checkpoint at the Quarterly Headmaster Review ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §5), alongside the Exposure Log (Doc 10 §6) and Interest Ledger ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §6).
