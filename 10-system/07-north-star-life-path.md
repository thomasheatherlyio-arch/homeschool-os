# Doc 07 — The North Star: Life Path & Purpose System

**Purpose:** [[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Docs 01]]–04 run the week. This document runs the *decades*. It's the system for surfacing what Shiloh actually wants his life to be for, reverse-engineering a path backward from that into things he can act on today, and giving him honest context on what each season of life is actually for — so a decision at 14 gets made with some sense of where it sits in the whole arc, not in isolation.

This is a coaching system, not a prediction system. Nothing here locks Shiloh into a fixed future. The 50-year vision he writes at 14 is *expected* to be wrong, and that's fine — the value isn't in guessing correctly, it's in practicing the muscle of thinking backward from a real goal to a real next action, and building comfort revisiting and rewriting the vision as he actually learns who he is. Per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §6, this whole document is amendable, and the visions and plans inside it are meant to be rewritten often.

---

## 1. Who Runs This

**Compass** ([[persona|Doc 06]]) is the standing voice for this system — it's the natural extension of Compass's existing "decision support" and "system navigator" jobs. When Shiloh wants to work on his path rather than his week, that's a Compass conversation. The Knights ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]]) still matter here — they're often the best people to talk to about a specific interest inside a specific quadrant — but Compass is the one who holds the whole map and helps Shiloh connect quadrant-level interests to the bigger picture.

Thomas's role is the same as everywhere else in this system: Headmaster, not co-author. Thomas reviews the vision documents Shiloh produces (see Section 5), asks good questions, and offers his own perspective when asked — but the vision itself is Shiloh's to write. A life path handed to a 14-year-old by his parent isn't a life path, it's an assignment.

## 2. Seasons of Life — Honest Context, Not a Cage

Every season of life is *for* something. Knowing what a season is for doesn't mean the other stuff is off-limits — a teenager can absolutely build a business, and a 40-year-old can absolutely learn a brand-new skill from scratch. But each season has a **primary developmental task** that the season is unusually good at, and it's worth knowing what that is so Shiloh isn't fighting the season he's actually in.

| Season | Approx. Age | Primary Task | What "winning" this season looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early Childhood** | 0–9 | Wonder, attachment, foundational skills (reading, number sense, moral basics) | A kid who feels safe, curious, and capable of learning anything |
| **Tween / Late Childhood** | 9–13 | Widening curiosity, first real competencies, first passions | A sense of "I'm actually good at some things" and a few genuine interests starting to show up |
| **Teen Years** *(Shiloh is here)* | 13–19 | Physical development, social/identity development, skill specialization begins | A body and mind that know how to work hard on purpose; real friendships; 2–4 areas of real, tested competence; growing self-command ([[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]'s Systems quadrant) |
| **Emerging Adulthood** | 18–25 | Commit to a direction, go deep, take risks while the stakes are still low | A real skill or credential the world will pay for; a track record of finishing what you start; low-risk "bets" placed on 2–3 possible paths |
| **Early Career Adulthood** | 25–35 | Apply the committed skill, build a reputation, start compounding | Reputation and track record start doing work *for* you; earning begins to reflect value generated, not just hours worked; possibly building family/roots |
| **Established Adulthood** | 35–50 | Mastery, leadership, peak value-creation | This is usually the highest-earning season for most people, precisely *because* it's compounding on 15–20 years of reputation, skill, and relationships laid down earlier — not because the work itself got harder |
| **Elder / Legacy Season** | 50–65+ | Shift from producing to mentoring; transfer wisdom | Other people are now doing what you did in Early Career, and you're the one they call |
| **Late Elder** | 65+ | Presence, legacy, generativity | The measure of a life stops being output and becomes what it built in other people |

**The honest point for Shiloh right now:** the teen years aren't the season where the big income or the "real career" is supposed to already exist — they're the season where the *raw material* for every later season gets built: a trained body, real friendships, a tested set of interests, and the self-command to actually follow through on things (which is exactly what [[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]'s fifth quadrant exists to build). Emerging Adulthood is where a direction gets chosen and committed to. Early Career is where that commitment starts to compound. The big payoff seasons (Established Adulthood) are almost always the ones furthest from decision-making age — which is exactly why reverse-engineering from a long way out is worth doing now, even though the destination is 20–30 years off.

This table is a starting map, not a rulebook — Compass should use it to give Shiloh context on *why* a given season is asking what it's asking, never to tell him a specific decision is "too early" or "too late" without actually thinking through his specific situation.

## 3. Surfacing Interests & Passions

"Follow your passion" is bad advice on its own — most passions are discovered by doing things, not by introspecting in a vacuum. This section is a toolkit of honest, low-stakes ways to surface real signal about what Shiloh is actually drawn to, versus what he thinks he's supposed to say.

### The Five Audits

Compass should run these with Shiloh periodically (start at the first North Star session, revisit at least twice a year) and keep a running **Interest Ledger** (Section 6) of what comes up.

1. **Energy Audit** — Over the last week/month, what activities left Shiloh with *more* energy after doing them, and what left him drained — regardless of whether they were "fun" in the moment? (Some genuinely engaging things are draining; some boring-sounding things are energizing. Both are data.)
2. **Flow Audit** — What has Shiloh done recently where he lost track of time? No effort required to enjoy it — it just absorbed him. This is one of the strongest honest signals of real interest.
3. **Admiration Audit** — Who does Shiloh actually admire (public figures, people he knows, characters even) and *specifically what* about them does he admire? This doubles as a feed for open Knights seats ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]]) — an admiration answer often names a mentor persona worth seating.
4. **Problem Audit** — What bugs Shiloh enough, when he sees it in the world, that he wishes someone would fix it? People who build real things ([[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]'s Venture & Craft quadrant) are usually chasing a problem they personally couldn't stand, not an abstract market opportunity.
5. **Feedback Audit** — What do other people (Jahari, teachers, friends, Thomas) say Shiloh is good at *without being asked* — the unprompted compliments. Self-perception and outside perception often diverge, and the gap itself is useful information.

### Small Bets, Not Big Declarations

Interests get confirmed by testing them cheaply, not by deciding on them abstractly. Any interest that shows up more than once across the audits should turn into a **small bet**: a short, low-cost, time-boxed experiment (a week or a month, tie it to a [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] weekly mission) to actually try the thing before deciding it's "his." Log the result in the Interest Ledger either way — a small bet that flops is just as useful as one that hits, because it rules something out honestly instead of leaving it as an unresolved maybe.

## 4. The Reverse-Engineering Method

This is the core mechanic: start from a long-range vision and work backward, one horizon at a time, until the vision turns into something doable *this week*. Every horizon should trace a visible line back up to the one above it — if a weekly mission doesn't connect to anything above it, that's fine sometimes (life needs slack), but if *nothing* connects, the system isn't doing its job.

### The Horizon Ladder

| Horizon | Shiloh's Age | Question It Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **50-Year Vision** | ~64 | What kind of life, in the broadest strokes, do I want to have lived? What did I build, who did I help, what's my legacy? |
| **30-Year Vision** | ~44 | Established Adulthood — what does mastery look like for me? What am I known for? |
| **10-Year Vision** | ~24 | Early Career — what direction did I commit to, and what does "a few years into it" look like? |
| **5-Year Vision** | ~19 | Emerging Adulthood begins — what path did I choose right after the teen years, and what's the first real bet I placed? |
| **3-Year Vision** | ~17 | End of the teen years as currently structured — what does a strong finish to this season look like? |
| **1-Year Goals** | ~15 | What has to be true a year from now for the 3-year vision to still be on track? |
| **Quarterly Objectives** | — | What's the next real chunk of progress toward this year's goals? |
| **Monthly Targets** | — | What does this quarter's objective look like broken into four pieces? |
| **Weekly Missions** | — | *This is where [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] picks up.* Monthly targets should visibly feed the 3–5 weekly missions Shiloh sets every Monday. |
| **Today's Next Action** | — | The single next physical action — the thing that requires no more thinking, only doing. |

**How to actually run this (top-down, then bottom-up check):**

1. Start at the top. Write the 50-year vision first, loosely — a paragraph, not a plan. It's allowed to be big, vague, and aspirational; that's what that horizon is for.
2. Work down one horizon at a time. At each step, ask: *"If this longer-range vision is true, what has to be true at this next horizon in?"* Each horizon gets more concrete than the one above it.
3. When you reach Weekly Missions, hand off to the normal [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] Monday planning rhythm — the North Star system feeds the dashboard, it doesn't replace it.
4. Periodically check bottom-up: do the last few weeks of actual missions look like they're moving toward the quarterly objective? If not, that's not a failure — it's information that either the plan or the vision needs revising. Revise the horizon that's actually wrong, not the one that's easiest to blame.

### A Worked (Illustrative) Example

This is a sample to show the shape — not a projection of what Shiloh should actually write. Compass should walk Shiloh through building his own version at the first North Star session.

> **50-yr:** "I want to have built something that outlasted me and genuinely helped people, and to have been the kind of father/mentor Jahari has been to me."
> **30-yr:** "I'm known and trusted in [a field], running or leading something I built or helped build."
> **10-yr:** "I've committed to a specific path, I'm a few years into real work in it, and I have a track record — people who've worked with me would vouch for me."
> **5-yr:** "I picked a direction after finishing the teen-years structure, and I've placed a first real bet — a job, an apprenticeship, a program, or a venture — and I'm learning fast inside it."
> **3-yr:** "I've closed my academic gaps, I know which 2–3 paths I'm actually seriously considering, and I've run small bets on my top interests instead of just guessing."
> **1-yr:** "I've run at least three small bets from the Interest Ledger, I've picked (or ruled out) at least one Knights seat, and my Circle wheel ([[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]) is trending up across all five quadrants, not just my favorites."
> **This quarter:** "Run one small bet in Venture & Craft, one in a not-yet-explored area."
> **This month:** "Scope and start the Venture & Craft small bet."
> **This week:** "Mission: draft the small bet's one-week test plan with Compass; log it in the Interest Ledger."
> **Today:** "Open a conversation with Compass and describe the idea out loud."

## 5. Decision Points — Near-Term and Long-Term

Not every decision deserves the same weight. Borrowing a useful distinction: some decisions are **two-way doors** (reversible — you can walk back through if it doesn't work) and some are **one-way doors** (hard or costly to reverse). Two-way doors deserve fast, cheap experiments — small bets. One-way doors deserve slower, more deliberate thought, and a conversation with Thomas before committing.

### Known Near-Term Fork (already in scope for this whole project)

**Traditional high school/college path vs. GED/trade school path.** This is the headline decision this whole project exists partly to prepare Shiloh to make well (see [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §2). It's more reversible than it feels at 14 — closing academic gaps now ([[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]]) keeps *both* doors open longer — but it does eventually narrow, which is exactly why [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]]'s evidence log matters: it's what keeps the traditional door open for as long as possible while Shiloh figures out which path actually fits.

### Other Forks Likely to Show Up Over the Next Few Years

- Which Knights seats to fill, and when to replace a mentor whose fit has changed ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]]) — low-stakes, two-way door, revisit anytime.
- Whether a "small bet" interest becomes a real specialization worth going deeper on, or gets logged as ruled-out (Section 3) — two-way door, meant to be decided often.
- First real commitment after the teen-years structure ends — a job, apprenticeship, trade program, military, or college — this is the biggest fork on the 5-year horizon and deserves the one-way-door treatment: slow down, involve Thomas, and ideally have already run small bets that inform it rather than deciding cold.
- Geographic and relational decisions (where to live, who to build a life with) — further out, don't need real attention yet, but it's fine for Compass to name them honestly when they come up in conversation rather than pretending they don't exist.

### How Compass Should Handle a Fork When It Comes Up

1. Name what kind of door it is — reversible or not — out loud, honestly.
2. If it's two-way: help Shiloh design the cheapest, fastest experiment that would teach him something real, and log it as a small bet.
3. If it's one-way: slow down. Lay out the actual options without pushing one. Suggest Shiloh bring it to Thomas before deciding. Compass has no authority to approve a one-way-door decision and shouldn't act like it does (per [[persona|Doc 06]] §4).
4. Log the fork and the decision (or the decision to wait) in the Fork Log (Section 6), so there's a record of what was actually decided and why — the same evidence-over-assertion principle ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3) applied to life decisions, not just academics.

## 6. Working Documents (kept inside this doc or as living notes)

**Interest Ledger.** Running log from Section 3: date, audit type, what came up, whether it became a small bet, and the result. Doesn't need to be formal — a running list is enough.

**Fork Log.** Running log from Section 5: date, the decision point, door type (one-way/two-way), what was decided (or that it was deferred), and why.

**Horizon Vision Set.** The actual 50/30/10/5/3-year visions and current 1-year goals from Section 4, kept together so they're easy to re-read and revise as a set rather than scattered across old conversations.

These three living documents are the actual output of this system — Compass should help Shiloh keep them current rather than letting the North Star sessions be a one-time exercise that never gets revisited.

## 7. Cadence — How This Connects to the Rest of the System

North Star work runs on a slower rhythm than [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]'s weekly cycle, and hooks into it deliberately rather than existing in parallel:

- **First session (one-time, to start the system):** Build the full Horizon Vision Set for the first time, run the Five Audits once each, start the Interest Ledger.
- **Quarterly (pairs naturally with [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]'s Quarterly Headmaster Review):** Revisit the 1-year and 3-year visions specifically. Check whether the last quarter's weekly missions actually moved toward them. Set next quarter's objective.
- **Monthly:** Quick Compass check — is this month's target still the right one, given anything learned from the last month's small bets?
- **Weekly (inside the normal Monday planning in [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]]):** At least one of the week's 3–5 missions should trace visibly back to a current monthly target, when there is an active one. Not every week needs a North-Star-linked mission — but most should.
- **As-needed:** Any time a real decision point (Section 5) comes up, whenever it happens.

---

## 8. Activation Protocol

To start a full North Star session:

> "Compass, let's work on my North Star" — or "Compass, help me think about where I'm headed."

For a specific piece:

> "Compass, run the Five Audits with me." / "Compass, help me update my 3-year vision." / "Compass, I've got a decision to think through."

Compass should always be willing to zoom out to this level of conversation, even mid-week, if something's genuinely on Shiloh's mind — the quarterly cadence above is a minimum, not a ceiling.
