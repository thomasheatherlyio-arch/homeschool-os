# Doc 09 — Diagnostic Assessment & Proficiency Rubric

**Purpose:** give Thomas and Shiloh a repeatable, low-stakes way to find Shiloh's actual starting point in any subject he's working on — and a shared rubric so "proficiency" means the same thing across subjects and stays comparable over time. This is the mechanism that makes [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3 ("Evidence over assertion") real for academics specifically, and it's the first entry point into the [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] evidence log for any subject.

---

## 1. Philosophy & Ground Rules

This is a **placement test, not an exam.** The whole point is to find the real starting line, not to produce a score Shiloh has to defend. That distinction matters given his history ([[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] §2) — the last time academic evaluation went badly for him, the damage was as much about the feeling of failing a test as it was about the content gap itself. This system is built to not repeat that.

Ground rules, non-negotiable:

- **No letter grades attached to a diagnostic result.** Results are reported as a Level (Section 3) and a plain-language "here's where we're starting, here's the target, here's the gap" — never a percentage or a grade.
- **Untimed.** Speed isn't what's being measured. Shiloh can take breaks mid-section whenever he wants.
- **One subject per sitting.** Never stack two diagnostics in the same day.
- **Ceiling testing, not full-length testing.** He does not grind through every question in a packet — see Section 4. The test stops climbing the moment it's found where the real edge of his knowledge is, so it never turns into a long string of failures.
- **Framed out loud, before he starts, every time:** *"This isn't graded. It just tells us where to start building so we're not wasting your time on stuff you already know, or throwing you into stuff you're not ready for yet. Wherever you land is just information."*
- **Administered by Thomas**, with a subject-expert Knight ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]]) looped in for scoring judgment calls where useful.

Results feed two places directly: the **[[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] evidence log** (as the first evidence entry for that subject) and the **[[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] dashboard** (seeding the initial Mind & Mastery quadrant score and shaping the next few weeks' missions).

**Why Reading, Writing, and Math first:** they're the foundational three everything else leans on, and per [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] §6 ("build the system first, load content second"), a lighter initial lift beats testing everything in his first week back into structured academics.

**Process for adding a subject to the queue:**

1. Shiloh and Thomas (or Shiloh and COLT, reviewed by Thomas) review this menu together — naturally fits a Monday planning session ([[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] §1).
2. Shiloh picks which queued subject comes next and roughly when — his choice, inside the structure, per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 2.
3. Before that subject is introduced as regular coursework, a diagnostic gets built for it using this doc's rubric and design principles (Sections 3–4), and this document's menu table gets updated to reflect it moving from "Queued" to assessed.
4. Diagnostics get scheduled roughly a week apart, never batched, so no single week turns into a testing gauntlet.

## 3. General Proficiency Rubric (subject-agnostic)

This rubric is what makes "Level 2 in Reading" and "Level 2 in Math" mean the same *kind* of thing, even though the content is totally different. Every diagnostic and every quarterly re-assessment gets scored against this table.

| Level | Label | Accuracy | Independence | Application / Transfer | Fluency & Retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Foundational Gaps | Struggles even with the easiest tier of grade-appropriate material | Needs the concept re-taught from scratch; can't yet work through it alone | Can't yet apply the skill outside the exact form it was taught in | No retained fluency — has to re-derive basics each time |
| **1** | Emerging | Gets it right sometimes, inconsistently, even on easier material | Needs prompting or a worked example nearby to get started | Recognizes the skill is relevant but can't reliably apply it to a new situation | Slow, effortful; the skill hasn't become automatic yet |
| **2** | Approaching | Mostly correct on material at or slightly below grade level; more errors as difficulty climbs | Can work independently on familiar problem types; still needs support on unfamiliar ones | Can apply the skill to a new situation with a hint or a nudge | Reasonably fluent on familiar material, hesitant on new material |
| **3** | Proficient | Consistently correct at grade level | Works independently without prompting | Applies the skill to new, unfamiliar situations without help | Fluent — doesn't have to think hard about the mechanics anymore |
| **4** | Advanced | Consistently correct above grade level | Independent and self-checking — catches its own mistakes | Transfers the skill into more complex or combined problems readily | Fully automatic; has room to teach or explain it to someone else |

**Grade-equivalent note:** the diagnostics below are organized into tiers that roughly track grade bands (a Tier 2 Math item is written at roughly a 6th–7th-grade level of difficulty, for instance), so a Level can be translated into a rough grade-equivalent estimate for the [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] record. But the number is a bookkeeping convenience — **the actual target is always "close this specific gap," not "hit a specific grade number."**

## 4. Assessment Design Principles — Ceiling Testing

Every diagnostic packet (Section 6 / the printable packet) is built in ascending **tiers**, each tier written at roughly one grade band of difficulty. The test is administered as a **ceiling test**, not a full-length exam:

1. **Start one tier below the best guess.** Given Shiloh's documented history ([[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] §2 — held back once, homeschooled in patches, a rocky partial 7th grade), the recommended default starting point for the initial v1.0 diagnostics is **Tier 2** (roughly 6th-grade-equivalent difficulty). Thomas can move the starting tier up or down in real time if it's obviously wrong within the first few questions.
2. **Climb while he's succeeding.** If Shiloh gets most of a tier right (roughly 70%+), move up one tier.
3. **Stop at the ceiling.** The moment a tier produces real struggle (roughly half or more missed, or visible frustration/guessing), stop climbing. That tier is the ceiling; **the last tier he was mostly successful in is his placement Level.**
4. **Confirm the floor only if needed.** If he struggles immediately even at the starting tier, step back one tier to confirm where solid ground actually is — this is data, not a bad outcome, and it's reported to Shiloh exactly that way (Section 1).
5. **Never push through a full tier of failure "to be thorough."** Once the ceiling is found, stop the section. There is no value in more wrong answers once the placement point is known, and real cost (per Section 1) to grinding through them.

## 5. The Process — Baseline and Ongoing Re-Assessment

**Step 1 — Confirm subject & schedule.** Per Section 2's selection process.

**Step 2 — Administer the diagnostic.** One sitting per subject, ceiling-tested per Section 4, roughly 30–45 minutes with breaks as needed. Use the printable packet (Section 7).

**Step 3 — Score against the rubric.** Assign a Level (0–4, Section 3) per subject, with a one- or two-line note on where exactly the ceiling showed up (e.g., "solid through fractions/decimals, ceiling hit at percent word problems").

**Step 4 — Record the baseline.** Log the result in the [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] evidence log and note it on the [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] dashboard; this becomes the initial Mind & Mastery quadrant reference point.

**Step 5 — Set a target and a gap-closing plan.** Thomas (with Shiloh) sets a target Level for the subject and feeds the specific skill gap identified in Step 3 into the next few weeks' missions ([[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] §1) — this is what actually turns a diagnostic into instruction instead of just a data point.

**Step 6 — Re-assess quarterly.** Tied to the Quarterly Headmaster Review ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §5). Quarterly re-assessment is **short and targeted**, not a full re-run of the ceiling test: start near the last placement tier, check one tier up and (if needed) one tier down, confirm or update the Level.

**Step 7 — Compare and log growth.** The comparison between baseline and each quarterly check becomes its own piece of evidence in [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] — proof of movement, which is the actual point of the whole exercise. If a subject hasn't moved, that's a prompt to adjust the plan ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 1: diagnose the system, not the kid), not a verdict on Shiloh.

## 8. Optional: Third-Party / Online Assessment Services

The packet in Section 7 is free, private, and built around this system's low-stakes philosophy — it doesn't need to be replaced. But there are two situations where an outside, nationally-normed tool is worth adding on top of it: (1) Thomas wants a second, independently-normed data point in the [[03-academic-tracker-tx-compliance|Doc 03]] evidence log — useful if a future school, program, or institution ever wants to see something beyond a homemade assessment — or (2) Shiloh's ongoing practice would benefit from an adaptive platform that re-diagnoses itself as he improves, rather than a one-time snapshot. Researched 2026-08-14; prices and offerings change, so confirm current pricing before purchasing.

**A. Nationally-normed standardized achievement tests (best for the evidence-log record)**

| Service | Format | Subjects | Price (as researched) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Renaissance STAR** (via [Homeschool Boss](https://homeschoolboss.com/renaissance-star/)) | Fully online, computer-adaptive | Reading + Math separately (~20–25 min each) | $75/student, one-time, 6-month unlimited-attempt access (individual/small group) | Fast, adaptive, gives a scaled score, percentile, and Lexile range — closest fit to this system's "quick and low-stakes" philosophy of the paid options |
| **NWEA MAP Growth** (via [Homeschool Boss](https://homeschoolboss.com/map-growth-pricing-homeschoolers/)) | Online, adaptive, untimed | Reading + Math bundled (Language/Science optional add-ons) | $60 one-time per season (1st student) or $162/yr for 3 sessions | Gives a RIT score designed specifically to track growth session-over-session — a natural fit for the quarterly re-assessment cadence in Section 5 |
| **Iowa Assessments (Form E) / Stanford 10 Online** (via [Seton Testing Services](https://www.setontesting.com/), [BJU Press Homeschool](https://www.bjupresshomeschool.com/testing/online), or the [University of Minnesota homeschool testing program](https://homeschool.umn.edu/faq)) | Online, full multi-subject battery | Reading, language, math, science, social studies | Contact vendor for current pricing — historically in the $35–65/subject-area range | The heavyweight, full-record option (this is what most co-ops/umbrella schools mean by "annual standardized testing"); more than a routine baseline needs, but the right tool if Thomas ever wants an official annual test on file |

**B. Free or low-cost adaptive diagnostics (better for ongoing gap-closing than a one-time record)**

| Service | Format | Subjects | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Math Mammoth Placement Tests** ([mathmammoth.com](https://www.mathmammoth.com/complete/placement_tests)) | Free downloadable PDF, self-scored | Math only, grades 1–8 | Free | Not adaptive, but organized by topic (fractions, geometry, etc.) — a strong, zero-cost cross-check for the math packet already built in Section 7 |
| **Khan Academy** ([khanacademy.org](https://www.khanacademy.org)) | Free, browser-based | Math (strong), some ELA/grammar | Free | No single formal "diagnostic," but Course Challenges/Unit Tests per grade-level course function as an informal placement tool, and Khan Academy doubles as the actual instruction once a gap is found |
| **IXL Real-Time Diagnostic** ([ixl.com/diagnostic](https://www.ixl.com/diagnostic/info)) | Online, adaptive, ~45 min/subject, continuously updates as he practices | Math + English Language Arts | Subscription required: ~$9.95/mo single subject or $15.95/mo combo (annual plans discounted) | Best of this group for ongoing tracking since the "diagnostic level" updates automatically rather than needing to be re-taken |
| **MobyMax** ([mobymax.com](https://www.mobymax.com/families)) | Online, adaptive | Math, reading, language, writing, science, social studies | ~$7.99/mo after a 30-day free trial | Broadest subject coverage of the low-cost options |
| **CTCMath** ([ctcmath.com](https://www.ctcmath.com/)) | Online, built-in placement tool | Math only | Free trial; homeschool discount available | Worth a look if Math is the subject Shiloh needs the most ongoing support in, since the placement tool and the curriculum are the same platform |

**Recommendation, if adding one of these:** Renaissance STAR is the best single addition — cheap, fast, untimed-feeling, and gives Thomas a real nationally-normed number for the evidence log without turning into the kind of long, heavy testing session this whole doc is designed to avoid. The free Math Mammoth tests are worth running alongside the homemade math packet at no cost, just as a second opinion. Full batteries (Iowa/Stanford) are worth keeping in mind for an eventual official annual record, but they're heavier than a routine baseline needs.
