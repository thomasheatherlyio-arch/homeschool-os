# Doc 10 — Work Exposure & Apprenticeship Explorer

**Purpose:** [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] says interests get confirmed by testing them cheaply, not by deciding on them abstractly (§3, "Small Bets, Not Big Declarations"). Doc 09 flags several trades worth exploring but treats them as "Interest Ledger candidates, not decisions." This doc is where that turns into action: a practical system for getting Shiloh real, in-person exposure to actual work — job shadows, informational interviews, helper gigs, and eventually apprenticeships — so a trade either earns its way onto his real short list or gets honestly ruled out, the same way every other interest in this system gets tested ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §3, [[01-holistic-quadrants-the-circle|Doc 01]]'s Venture & Craft quadrant).

This is reconnaissance, not enlistment. Nothing here commits Shiloh to a trade path over the traditional one — [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §5 already names that as the biggest one-way door on his 5-year horizon, and the whole point of starting exposure now, at 14, is to have real data by the time that door actually needs to close.

*Researched 2026-08-14. Labor law and program details can shift and vary by city/employer — reverify anything decision-critical (especially hour limits and hazardous-occupation lists) directly with TWC/DOL before a specific placement.*

---

## 1. The Exposure Ladder

Not everything on this ladder is available at every age. The ladder below is ordered from "available right now" to "available once the legal doors open" — Shiloh doesn't need to wait for the top rung to start; the bottom two rungs are open today.

| Rung | What it is | Roughly when it opens for Shiloh | Legal/logistical notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Informational interview** | A 20–30 min conversation with someone working the trade — not asking for a job, just asking how they got in and what the work is actually like | **Now (age 14)** | No labor law involved at all — this is a conversation, not employment |
| **2. Job shadow** | Spending part or all of a workday alongside a tradesperson, observing real work | **Now (age 14)**, with the host's/employer's permission | Not employment, so youth labor law doesn't apply directly — but a jobsite may have its own insurance/liability rules for visitors; always ask, don't assume |
| **3. Volunteer/helper work** | Unpaid or informal help on a specific project (a family friend's garage build, a church workday, a small job) | **Now, case-by-case** | Same logic as job shadowing but hands-on; a parent-owned or family-friend business can involve Shiloh more freely than a formal employer relationship can |
| **4. Limited paid work** | Actual part-time paid employment in a non-hazardous setting (retail, food service, gas stations are the categories Texas names for this age band) | **Age 14–15**, narrow occupation list | TX and federal hour limits apply (Section 2) — this rung is more about building a real work-ethic/reliability track record than trade exposure specifically |
| **5. Pre-apprenticeship / CTE trade coursework** | Structured trade coursework short of a formal registered apprenticeship — high school CTE programs, community-college trade certificate intro courses, employer-run "youth apprenticeship" pipelines | **Typically age 16+**, program-dependent | Availability for homeschoolers varies by ISD and by community college — worth a direct call to a local CTE office or community-college workforce department when this rung becomes relevant, rather than assuming access |
| **6. Registered apprenticeship** | The real thing — paid, structured, "earn while you learn," ends in a portable credential | **Minimum age 16 for most trades; 18 for some hazardous ones** (per TWC) | This is the credential Doc 09 §4 discusses in detail as the actual entry point into a trade career |

**The honest read for right now:** at 14, Shiloh's whole job is Rungs 1–3. That's not a consolation prize — informational interviews and job shadows are actually the highest-signal, lowest-cost way to test a trade interest, because they cost nothing and take an afternoon. A registered apprenticeship is a two-to-four-year commitment; nobody should walk into one without having already talked to and watched people who do the work.

## 2. Texas Legal Guardrails (know these before arranging anything)

### Youth employment (paid work)

| Age | What's allowed |
|---|---|
| **Under 14** | Employment is generally illegal, with narrow exceptions: work for a parent/legal custodian's business, newspaper delivery (11+), school-supervised work-study, agricultural work, and authorized child performer roles |
| **14–15** | Limited to occupations in retail, food service, and gas stations. Texas caps hours at 8/day and 48/week, no work before 5 a.m. or past 10 p.m. on school nights. Federal law is tighter during the school year: max 3 hrs/school day, 18 hrs/school week, and only between 7 a.m.–7 p.m. (7 a.m.–9 p.m. in summer) |
| **16–17** | No hour restrictions; any job except those on the hazardous-occupations list (explosives, mining, logging/sawmill, roofing, most power-driven machinery without an apprentice/student-learner exemption) |

**14–15-year-olds are barred from:** manufacturing/processing, operating power-driven machinery, driving or riding as a driver's helper, cooking involving open flames/deep fryers, work in freezers/meat coolers, and most loading/unloading/warehouse work. This is exactly why the trade menu in Section 3 leans on job shadowing and informational interviews rather than "get Shiloh a job on a job site" — most real trade *work* at this age is legally off the table; real trade *exposure* isn't.

**Exception worth knowing:** a business owned by a parent or legal custodian can employ Shiloh at any age for non-hazardous work. If a family friend or a Knight ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]]) owns a trade business, that relationship may open doors the general rules don't.

### Registered apprenticeships specifically

Per Texas Workforce Commission, the minimum age to enter a **registered apprenticeship** is generally **16**, though some programs — anything touching hazardous work — require **18**. This lines up with Doc 09's GED-eligibility timeline: the same 16–18 window is when both the GED door and the formal-apprenticeship door open, which is part of why Doc 09 calls that age range the point the two-path fork actually starts to narrow.

## 3. The Trade Menu — Interest Ledger Candidates

This is a starting list, not a ranking. Doc 09 §4 already flagged HVAC, electrical, welding, and aircraft/wind-turbine technician work as reasonable-to-strong demand and pay in Texas. The list below adds a few more common entry points so Shiloh has real breadth to run the Five Audits ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §3) against — Energy, Flow, Admiration, Problem, and Feedback — rather than picking from a short list by default.

| Trade | What the day-to-day actually looks like | Typical entry path |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrical** | Wiring buildings, reading blueprints, troubleshooting circuits, code compliance | JATC (Joint Apprenticeship & Training Committee) programs, e.g. South Texas Electrical JATC; TDLR licenses electricians in Texas |
| **HVAC/R** | Installing and repairing heating/cooling/refrigeration systems, diagnostics, customer-facing service calls | Community-college certificate programs, employer-sponsored apprenticeships |
| **Welding** | Fabrication, structural/pipe welding, reading weld specs, often physically demanding and outdoor/industrial | Community-college or private trade-school certificate (6.5–10 months per Doc 09), employer-run youth apprenticeship pipelines exist (e.g., United Alloy's weld youth apprenticeship) |
| **Plumbing** | Pipefitting, fixture installation, diagnosing leaks/blockages, code compliance | JATC/union apprenticeship, TDLR licensing |
| **Automotive/diesel technician** | Diagnostics, repair, increasingly computer-driven troubleshooting | Community-college programs, dealership-sponsored apprenticeships |
| **Carpentry/construction trades** | Framing, finish work, reading plans, project sequencing | Home-builder trade associations (e.g., ABC Central Texas, ABC South Texas) run registered apprenticeship pipelines |
| **Aircraft/wind-turbine technician** | Maintenance and inspection of aircraft systems or turbine mechanical/electrical systems, often outdoor/at-height for wind | FAA-approved A&P schools (aircraft) or community-college wind-energy technician programs |

**How this connects to [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]]:** every trade on this list (or any trade Shiloh names that isn't) is a candidate for the Interest Ledger. A trade only earns a spot on Shiloh's real short list after it survives at least one Rung 1 or Rung 2 exposure (Section 1) — a conversation or a shadow day — logged honestly in the Exposure Log below, whether it confirms the interest or rules it out.

## 4. How to Actually Get Exposure

### Informational interviews (Rung 1)

The ask is small and most tradespeople say yes to it. A workable script:

> "Hi, I'm Shiloh, I'm 14 and homeschooled, and I'm trying to learn about different trades before I decide anything. Would you have 20 minutes sometime to tell me how you got into [trade] and what a normal day actually looks like? I'd come to you, or we could do it by phone."

Good sources for a first contact: Knights network ([[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]] — Jahari or any IRL mentor may know someone), family friends, a local trade-association chapter (ABC, JATC locals), or simply asking a tradesperson doing visible work in the neighborhood (a lineman, an HVAC tech on a service call) if they'd be open to a short conversation once the job's done.

**Questions worth asking every time (keep these consistent so answers are comparable across trades):**
- How did you get into this trade, and what would you do differently?
- What does a normal week actually look like — hours, physical demands, indoor/outdoor, solo/team?
- What's the actual pay range starting out vs. after a few years?
- What's the worst part of the job nobody tells you about?
- Who would you introduce me to if I wanted to shadow a day?

That last question is how Rung 1 turns into Rung 2.

### Job shadows (Rung 2)

Once someone's open to it, confirm logistics directly with Thomas involved (Headmaster sign-off, per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §4 — this is a real-world activity outside the home, not a self-directed one): whose jobsite, what hours, what to wear, any safety gear needed, and whether the host's employer needs a visitor waiver signed. Log it in the Exposure Log the same day, while details are fresh.

### Volunteer/helper work (Rung 3)

Same process as a job shadow, but with actual hands-on tasks. This is the highest-value rung for testing "do I actually like doing this, not just watching it" — worth prioritizing over Rung 2 once a trade has survived one shadow and is still interesting.

## 5. Small Bet Design for Work Exposure

Per [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §3, any interest that shows up more than once should turn into a time-boxed small bet. For work exposure, a small bet looks like:

> "This month: run one informational interview and one job shadow in [trade], and log both in the Exposure Log — result either way (confirmed interest / ruled out / unclear, try again)."

This ties naturally into a [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] weekly mission — reaching out for an informational interview, or preparing questions for a shadow day, is a perfectly good "next action" to put on the dashboard. A shadow day with no reflection afterward is a fun afternoon, not evidence — same principle [[02-knights-of-the-round-table|Doc 02]] applies to mentor sessions.

## 6. Exposure Log

Kept here (or as a living note, same as [[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]]'s working documents) — one row per Rung 1+ contact:

| Date | Trade | Rung | Who/where | What happened | Result (confirmed / ruled out / unclear) | Feeds Interest Ledger? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *(example)* 2026-09-02 | HVAC | Informational interview | Family friend's HVAC tech | 25-min call, learned about apprenticeship-to-license timeline | Unclear — wants to see a shadow day | Logged |

This log is the paper trail for [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] Principle 3 (evidence over assertion) applied to career exploration — by the time a real fork shows up ([[07-north-star-life-path|Doc 07]] §5, "first real commitment after the teen-years structure ends"), there should be an actual record of what Shiloh tried, not just a guess about what he'd like.

---

## 7. Cadence

- **Now through age 15:** Rungs 1–3 only. Target a light, sustainable pace — one informational interview or shadow attempt roughly every 4–6 weeks is plenty; this shouldn't crowd out the rest of the [[04-student-dashboard|Doc 04]] weekly rhythm.
- **Age 15–16:** Add limited paid work (Rung 4) as a track-record builder if a role fits the occupation/hour limits, independent of whether it's trade-specific — reliability and work ethic are their own evidence.
- **Age 16+:** Revisit Rungs 5–6 directly — check current CTE/dual-credit access as a homeschooler, and treat any trade that's survived multiple exposures as a real registered-apprenticeship candidate, per Doc 09 §4–5.
- **Tie-in:** Quarterly Headmaster Review ([[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §5) is a natural checkpoint to review the Exposure Log alongside the Interest Ledger and Fork Log.

---

## 8. Activation Protocol

To start this track:

> "Compass, let's find someone I can talk to about [trade]." / "Compass, help me write an informational-interview outreach message." / "Compass, log today's shadow day in the Exposure Log."

Thomas's sign-off is needed before any actual outside-the-home placement (job shadow, volunteer work, paid work) happens — Compass can help Shiloh prepare and think it through, but per [[00-governing-charter-and-toc|Doc 00]] §4, real-world logistics and safety are a Headmaster call.
