# Doc 14 — The Kitchen: Culinary Studio, Menu Design & Food Systems Course

**Purpose:** Shiloh has chosen to spend his afternoons on chef/cooking skills — menu creation, researching where food actually comes from, and sourcing ingredients. This doc turns that choice into a real course: a structured but self-paced curriculum that treats cooking as a legitimate career/life-skill track (per Doc 10's exposure-ladder logic) *and* a vehicle for real Texas-curriculum subject credit (per Doc 03) — math, health, science, and more, taught through a kitchen instead of a workbook, per Thomas's direction that this count academically wherever it honestly can.

---

## 1. Why This Fits This System

Every recipe is a math problem (scaling, ratios, unit conversion, costing); every dish is a science experiment (heat, chemistry, biology); every ingredient has a supply chain (sourcing = social-studies/civics); a planned menu is executive-function practice (mise en place = Systems & Self-Command); a finished, served meal is real evidence (Doc 00 Principle 3). Food service is also one of the specific occupation categories Texas permits for 14–15-year-olds (Doc 10 §2), so this is a real Interest Ledger candidate, not just an elective.

## 2. Quadrant Map

Anchored in **Venture & Craft**, pulling hard into **Mind & Mastery**. Kitchen fundamentals/safety → Systems & Self-Command; kitchen math → Mind & Mastery; food science/nutrition → Mind & Mastery; food-ecosystem/sourcing research → Mind & Mastery + Spirit & Character (civics); menu design/costing → Venture & Craft; cuisine/culture/history → Mind & Mastery; capstone Menu Night → all five.

## 3. Texas Curriculum Tie-In (per Doc 03)

Mathematics (recipe scaling, unit conversion, costing) · Reading (recipes, food-science sources) · Spelling & Grammar (written menus, reports) · Good Citizenship (food-ecosystem/sourcing research, supporting local producers) · Science (food science, safety/microbiology) · Health & PE (nutrition, label reading) · History/Social Studies (culinary history unit) · Executive Function (planning a sourcing trip, running mise en place, executing a multi-course dinner on schedule).

## 4. Course Structure — 8 Units + Capstone

1. **Kitchen Fundamentals & Food Safety** — knife skills, stove/oven safety, sanitation, mise en place → a self-built safety checklist + a dish cooked start-to-finish.
2. **Kitchen Math & Measurement** — scaling, unit conversion, ratios → conversion worksheet + a recipe actually cooked at a new scale.
3. **Food Science & Nutrition** — Maillard reaction, emulsification, fermentation, macros → a mini-experiment write-up + a nutrition breakdown of one meal.
4. **The Food Ecosystem** — supply chains, farm→distributor→store/market→plate → a research report tracing 2-3 ingredients to their source.
5. **Sourcing Ingredients** — grocery vs. farmers market vs. CSA, budgeting a real grocery run → a real sourcing trip, receipts, a reflection.
6. **Menu Design & Recipe Development** — a coherent 3-course menu, recipe testing/iteration → a finished written menu.
7. **Food Costing, Pricing & Running a Kitchen Like a Business** — cost-per-plate math, target food-cost % → a costed-out version of the Unit 6 menu.
8. **Cuisine, Culture & Culinary History** — one cuisine's origin/evolution → a short research writeup/presentation.
**Capstone — Menu Night:** Shiloh plans, sources, and cooks a full multi-course menu solo for the family; optional stretch goal is a real small pop-up dinner priced like a business.

## 5–12. Skill Placement, Weekly Rhythm, Small Bet/Contract, Sourcing Track, Career Tie-In, Studio Tie-In

Skill placement is an honest self-check (new/comfortable/confident) that compresses early units accordingly. Plugs into the normal Doc 04 weekly rhythm (Monday mission, afternoon kitchen time, Friday review/logging). First small bet: run Units 1–3 over 3-4 weeks, then log an honest Interest Ledger verdict. Units 4-5 (the food ecosystem/sourcing track) is a real Doc 10 Rung 1 informational-interview opportunity with a farmer or vendor. Career tie-in: Rung 1 (chef/caterer interview) → Rung 2 (kitchen shadow) → Rung 4 (food service is TX-permitted at 14-15) → Doc 13 tie-in (a "family chef" paid Extras Menu line). Optional Studio tie-in: any unit experiment, vendor interview, or Menu Night itself can double as Doc 11 content.

## 13a. Addendum — "Opening Night": The First Small Bet, Concretized (added 2026-08-24)

The Section 7 small bet became a scoped **6-week, 12-session "open your own restaurant" project** — a fillable Word/Google Docs workbook (delivered directly to Thomas) that Shiloh fills in twice a week. Structure: Wk1 Find Your Concept, Wk2 Build the Menu & Learn the Rules (TX food-handler/licensing research), Wk3 Where Your Food Actually Comes From, Wk4 Learning From a Real Founder (a recorded informational interview with Naomi Seifter, founder of Picnik, Austin), Wk5 Price It Like a Real Business, Wk6 Open Your Doors (capstone). Three videos are due across the six weeks (concept pitch, the Seifter interview, the Opening Night capstone dinner). A Texas food-handler certificate (no state minimum age) is a stretch-goal checkbox. The workbook's final page is an Interest-Ledger-style confirmed/ruled-out/unclear reflection.
