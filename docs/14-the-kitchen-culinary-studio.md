# Doc 14 — The Kitchen: Culinary Studio, Menu Design & Food Systems Course

**Purpose:** Shiloh has chosen to spend his afternoons on chef/cooking skills — menu creation, researching where food actually comes from, and sourcing ingredients. This doc turns that choice into a real course: a structured but self-paced curriculum that treats cooking as a legitimate career/life-skill track (per Doc 10's exposure-ladder logic) *and* a vehicle for real Texas-curriculum subject credit (per Doc 03) — math, health, science, and more, taught through a kitchen instead of a workbook, per Thomas's direction that this count academically wherever it honestly can.

---

## 1. Why This Fits This System

Doc 03 §6 already names the strategy this doc leans on hardest: *"Route content through his real interests where possible... motivation follows relevance."* Cooking is an unusually good vehicle for that, because almost nothing about it is single-subject:

- **Every recipe is a math problem** — scaling, ratios, unit conversion, and (once menu costing starts) real arithmetic with real financial stakes.
- **Every dish is a science experiment** — heat, chemistry, and biology are happening whether or not anyone names them, which makes this a natural home for Doc 11 §2.5-style edutainment ("why does bread actually rise?").
- **Every ingredient has a supply chain** — sourcing research is genuine social-studies/civics material (Doc 03's "good citizenship" subject), not a stretch.
- **A planned menu is executive-function practice in disguise** — mise en place (literally "everything in its place") is Doc 01's Systems & Self-Command quadrant, wearing an apron.
- **A finished, served meal is real evidence** — per Doc 00 Principle 3, nobody has to take Shiloh's word for it that he learned something; a plated menu, a costing sheet, and a family's dinner are the paper trail.

This also slots directly into Doc 10's exposure-ladder logic: food service is one of the specific occupation categories Texas actually permits for 14–15-year-olds (Doc 10 §2), which means "chef" isn't just a fun unit — it's a real, testable career candidate for the Interest Ledger (Doc 07 §3), the same way HVAC or welding is.

## 4. Course Structure — Units

Suggested pace: roughly **1–2 weeks per unit**, running in afternoon sessions (however many afternoons/how-long-each fits the weekly dashboard — see Section 6). Units build on each other but Shiloh can move faster through anything that's already comfortable — see Section 5.

| Unit | Focus | Key skills | Deliverable / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Kitchen Fundamentals & Food Safety** | Knife skills, stove/oven safety, sanitation, mise en place | Basic knife cuts, safe heat handling, cross-contamination prevention, "clean as you go" | A self-built kitchen safety checklist; a simple dish cooked start-to-finish following it |
| **2. Kitchen Math & Measurement** | The math embedded in every recipe | Scaling a recipe up/down, unit conversion, ratios, reading a scale | Conversion worksheet; one recipe doubled or halved and actually cooked at the new scale |
| **3. Food Science & Nutrition** | What's actually happening chemically, and what's in the food | Maillard reaction, emulsification, fermentation/yeast, macros, reading nutrition labels | One "why does this work" mini-experiment write-up (e.g., yeast bread rise test); nutrition breakdown of one meal |
| **4. The Food Ecosystem — Where Food Actually Comes From** | Supply chains: farm → distributor → store/market → plate | Researching how a specific ingredient reaches a kitchen, local vs. industrial agriculture, seasonality | A short written research report tracing 2–3 ingredients back to their source |
| **5. Sourcing Ingredients — Smart Shopping & Farm-to-Table** | Turning Unit 4's research into real practice | Comparing grocery store vs. farmers market vs. a CSA/local farm, reading labels, budgeting a real grocery run | A real sourcing trip (with Thomas), receipts, and a short reflection on cost/quality/origin tradeoffs |
| **6. Menu Design & Recipe Development** | Creative, Venture & Craft core | Building a coherent menu (not just random dishes), recipe testing and iteration, plating/presentation | A written, finished 3-course menu with at least one original or meaningfully-adapted recipe |
| **7. Food Costing, Pricing & Running a Kitchen Like a Business** | The business side of Venture & Craft | Cost-per-plate math, target food-cost percentage, pricing a dish like it had to sell | A costed-out version of the Unit 6 menu, priced as if it were being sold |
| **8. Cuisine, Culture & Culinary History** | History/social-studies credit | Where a chosen cuisine came from, how it evolved, what it says about the place/people | Short research writeup or presentation on one cuisine, feeding into the capstone menu's theme |
| **Capstone — Menu Night** | Everything above, live | Planning, sourcing, cooking, and serving a real menu solo | See Section 9 |

## 5. Skill Placement — Start Where He Actually Is

Before Unit 1 locks in, run a quick, honest self-check with Shiloh (COLT can run this conversationally) rather than assuming a level:

- **New to real cooking** → start Unit 1 at full length, with Thomas supervising knife work and stove/oven use directly until comfort is demonstrated, not assumed.
- **Comfortable making simple meals solo already** → Unit 1 can compress to a safety-checklist review plus one demonstration dish, then move faster into Units 2–3.
- **Already cooks regularly and confidently** → compress Units 1–2, and let him run more of Units 4–5 (research, sourcing trip planning) independently, with Thomas checking the final plan rather than co-planning it.

This is a home-kitchen course under direct parental supervision, not paid employment — Doc 10 §2's age-based restrictions (open flame, deep fryers, etc.) govern *outside paid work*, not cooking at home with Thomas present. Worth knowing anyway, because it's exactly the kind of real-world detail that'll matter later if food service ever becomes an actual Rung 4 job (Section 10).

## 8. The Food Ecosystem & Sourcing Track (Units 4–5, in depth)

This is the piece Shiloh specifically asked for, so it's worth spelling out rather than leaving it as one table row:

**Research questions worth chasing in Unit 4** (pick 2–3 ingredients to trace end-to-end): Where is this actually grown/raised? How does it get from there to a Texas grocery store or market? What's the difference between how it reaches a big chain store versus a local farmers market? Who actually gets paid along that chain, and how much of the price is the ingredient itself versus transport, processing, and markup?

**Turning it into practice in Unit 5:** a real trip — a grocery store, a farmers market, and (if accessible) a local farm or CSA pickup — comparing the same or similar ingredients across all three: price, freshness, where it's actually from, and how far it traveled. This is also a natural informational-interview opportunity per Doc 10 §4 — a farmer or market vendor is a legitimate Rung 1 contact, with the same five-question script Doc 10 already uses, adapted to food ("How did you get into this, what does a normal week look like, what's the part nobody tells you about?").

## 9. Capstone — Menu Night

**Required minimum:** Shiloh plans, sources, and cooks a full multi-course menu solo for the family — this is the "Build & Make" example already named in Doc 11 §4's activity menu, now given a full course behind it. Menu gets designed (Unit 6), costed (Unit 7), and themed around what he learned in Unit 8's cuisine research.

**Stretch goal (optional, real Venture & Craft swing):** turn it into an actual small pop-up — invite a few extra people (family friends), price it like Unit 7's costing exercise for real, and run the whole night like a one-time small food business. This is the kind of bigger bet Doc 07 §3 describes — worth doing only if the required minimum went well and Shiloh wants to keep going.

Either version gets filmed if Shiloh wants it to double as Studio content (Section 11).

## 13a. Addendum — The First Small Bet: "Opening Night" (6-Week Restaurant Course)

*Added 2026-08-24, per Thomas.* The Section 7 small bet became a concrete, scoped project rather than staying abstract: Shiloh's whole course got reframed as **opening his own restaurant**, start to finish, over 6 weeks. This addendum records the shape of that concrete run so future sessions have it, even though the actual fillable workbook lives outside the project (delivered to Thomas as a Word document built to open cleanly in Google Docs — he uploads it to Drive and it converts automatically — which Shiloh fills in twice a week).

**Structure:** 6 weeks, 2 work sessions/week (12 sessions total), 3 videos due every 2 weeks, one real capstone dinner.

| Week | Focus | Ties to Section 4 units |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find Your Concept — brainstorm, competitive research | Menu design groundwork |
| 2 | Build the Menu & Learn the Rules — draft menu v1, TX food-handler/health-inspection/licensing research | Units 6, 1 |
| 3 | Where Your Food Actually Comes From — sourcing plan, recipe test #1 | Units 4–5 |
| 4 | Learning From a Real Founder — interview prep + the interview itself | Doc 10 exposure ladder |
| 5 | Price It Like a Real Business — menu v2, food costing & pricing | Units 2, 7 |
| 6 | Open Your Doors — brand/experience, run-of-show, capstone | Units 6–7, capstone (Section 9) |

**The three videos:** Video 1 (end of Wk 2) — a 2–3 min pitch of the restaurant concept. Video 2 (end of Wk 4) — a recorded informational interview (Doc 10 §4 process) with **Naomi Seifter, founder of Picnik** (Austin; started 2013 from a food trailer, built it into a full restaurant and retail brand, and — as of July 2026 — is relaunching Picnik into a new concept, which makes her mid-decision on exactly the kind of choices Shiloh is practicing, not just recounting an old story). Video 3 (end of Wk 6) — the capstone Opening Night dinner, cooked and served to the family on camera, plus a closing reflection.

**Also folded in:** the Texas food-handler-certificate fact from research earlier in this project (no state minimum age — Shiloh could realistically earn a real certificate during Week 2) became a stretch-goal checkbox rather than just a fact.

**Closes the loop back to Doc 07:** the workbook's final page is a Interest Ledger-style reflection — confirmed / ruled out / still unclear — so this small bet actually resolves one way or another rather than fading out.
