# Doc 16 — The Panel: Electrical Engineering & Electrician Career Course

**Purpose:** A 3-month (12-week) activity-based course introducing Shiloh to electrical engineering fundamentals and the electrician trade — built like Docs 14/15, routing Texas-required and standard-practice subjects (Doc 03) through hands-on work, and doubling as a genuine Doc 10 Exposure Ladder candidate. Leads with a live, well-documented demand spike: the AI data-center buildout (Section 8).

## 1–3. Why This Fits / Quadrant Map / Texas Tie-In

Anchored in **Venture & Craft**, pulling hard into **Mind & Mastery**. Every circuit is an algebra problem (Ohm's Law V=I×R, Watt's Law P=V×I); every panel is a systems-thinking exercise; every code requirement is a civics lesson (why the NEC/licensing exists — public safety). Covers Mathematics (circuit math, wire-gauge/ampacity, voltage-drop, bidding math), Reading (NEC, blueprints, licensing statutes), Spelling & Grammar (safety checklist, business-plan pitch), Good Citizenship (why licensing exists), Science (physics of electricity, circuits, electromagnetism), History/Social Studies (electrification history), Health & PE (jobsite safety), Executive Function (planning the multi-week project and pitch).

## 4. Course Structure — 12 Weeks

Wk1 Why Electricians, Right Now (orientation + demand story) · Wk2 Electrical Safety & Tools · Wk3 Engineering Fundamentals I — Physics of Electricity (Ohm's/Watt's Law, series/parallel, AC/DC) · Wk4 Engineering Fundamentals II — Systems Thinking (home electrical system, safety margins) · Wk5 The Math of the Trade (wire-gauge/ampacity, voltage-drop, conduit-fill) · Wk6 Reading the Language — Blueprints & the NEC · Wk7 Hands-On Circuits — Build & Test (low-voltage trainer kit/breadboard, multimeter) · Wk8 Residential Systems Case Study (unenergized home panel walkthrough with Thomas) · Wk9 The Licensing Ladder Deep Dive · Wk10 From Journeyman to Founder (business math, bidding) · Wk11 Exposure & Research (a real Doc 10 Rung 1 informational interview) · Wk12 Capstone — Build, Test, Pitch.

**Hard safety rule regardless of skill level:** this is a low-voltage/fundamentals course, never live household current (120V/240V). Week 8's panel walkthrough is observation-only, breakers off, Thomas present.

## 8. Why Now — The AI/Data-Center Demand Boom

The core driver: the AI buildout runs on data centers, which run on enormous electrical infrastructure. ~$700B in 2026 AI infrastructure spend across five major tech companies. McKinsey projects **130,000 additional electricians needed 2023-2030** just for AI infrastructure. Data-center construction workers averaged **$81,800/yr** in 2026 (~32% above standard commercial pay); some under-30 electricians on Plano, TX data-center jobs earned **$240k-$280k/yr**. Honest catch: data-center work is often project-based (18-24 month builds), so a portable license (Section 9) matters more than any single high-paying gig.

## 9. The Licensing Ladder (TDLR, Texas)

Electrical Apprentice (min age 16, $20 fee, works under supervision while logging hours) → Residential Wireman (4,000 hrs OJT) → Journeyman Electrician (8,000 hrs OJT + exam, ~4-5 yrs) → Master Electrician (12,000 hrs OJT + min. 2 yrs as journeyman + exam, ~7-10+ yr total climb) → Electrical Contractor business license ($110). Real entry point at 16: South Texas Electrical JATC. Shiloh can't register yet at 14, but dual-credit CTE coursework (per Doc 09 §6 Option A) is available from 9th grade, contingent on college-readiness benchmarks this course's math/reading work directly builds toward.

## 10. From Journeyman to Founder — Income by Stage

Apprentice ~$21k-$56k · Journeyman ~$38.5k-$104.5k (median ~$68k) · Master ~$80k avg, top 10% exceed $100k · Business owner: personal draw commonly $60k-$150k, business can gross $500k+/yr. Owning a company requires the separate Electrical Contractor license plus insurance/bonding/hiring — a real leadership skill, not just technical. Staying a journeyman/master working for someone else is a completely legitimate, well-paying outcome on its own.

## 11. Capstone — Build, Test, Pitch

Two parts: (1) design/build/test a real low-voltage circuit on a trainer kit or breadboard, documented with photos/diagram/multimeter readings; (2) a real business-plan pitch ("[Shiloh's Name] Electric") using Section 9's actual TDLR hour requirements and Section 10's actual income ranges — not a fantasy pitch deck.

## 5–7, 12–15. Skill Placement, Weekly Rhythm, Small Bet/Contract, Career Tie-In, Studio Tie-In

Skill placement compresses early weeks for a student already comfortable with algebra or already tinkering with electronics. First small bet: run Weeks 1-4, then log an honest Interest Ledger verdict on whether the math/physics side (vs. just the AI-demand headline) actually holds his attention. Career tie-in follows Doc 10's Rung 1 (Wk11 interview) → Rung 2 (shadow) → Rung 5 (dual-credit CTE) → Rung 6 (registered apprenticeship at 16) ladder.
