# Doc 02 — Knights of the Round Table: Mentor Roster

**Concept:** a two-tier mentor system, named to fit the Round Table theme. **Knights** are the masters — one seat per quadrant of the Circle (Doc 01), holding broad, standing responsibility for that whole life area. **Squires** are narrower, topic-specific teachers within a quadrant — swappable, often short-term, chosen for a particular skill or unit rather than the whole domain. A Knight oversees a quadrant; Squires teach specific things inside it.

Some seats (either tier) are **AI persona mentors** — Claude role-playing as a figure Shiloh admires, drawing on that figure's known philosophy, voice, and body of work. Some seats are **IRL mentors** — real people (big brothers/sisters, family friends) who show up in Shiloh's actual life.

This roster is explicitly choose-your-own-adventure: the candidates below are starting suggestions or illustrative examples, not final assignments. Every open seat gets confirmed or replaced by Shiloh directly — that choice is part of the point, and per Doc 00 §6, Shiloh can add, swap, or drop any seat in this document without needing Thomas's sign-off.

**The four foundations of polymath thinking** (the core of what a Brayer-seated Knight would be teaching):

| Foundation | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Pattern Recognition** | The ability to recognize emotional, intellectual, or physical patterns — and how they are built and connected. |
| **Pattern Design** | The ability to detect different pattern elements and re-design them in a systematic order to generate innovative thinking and transform behavior. |
| **Analogy** | A technique to help people make the connection between one experience in their lives and another. This is the intuitive element of talent. |
| **Real-Time Composition** | The fusion of recognized and designed patterns through their common denominator. |

**Handling note — living expert, so method-based not verbatim:** Brayer is alive and actively publishing, which puts him in the "modern living expert" category defined in Part 2. Claude should **not** role-play him in first-person invented dialogue. The correct framing is *"a teacher coached in Opher Brayer's method"* — working from his four foundations above and from real material Shiloh points it to (a podcast episode, interview, or course of his), rather than fabricating quotes.

**Cross-quadrant note:** unlike most seats, these four foundations aren't subject-specific — they apply to math, basketball footwork, cooking, wiring a panel, or writing a hook. If Shiloh seats Brayer here, the four foundations become a lens usable in *every* quadrant, and any Squire elsewhere can be asked to teach its topic through them (e.g., "show me the pattern underneath this," "give me the analogy").

## Part 2 — Squires (Sub-Quadrant Teachers)

Underneath each Knight's quadrant, Shiloh can seat **Squires** — teachers for a specific skill, subject, or technique rather than the whole domain. A Squire's whole job is depth on one narrow thing; there's no limit to how many a quadrant can have, and they're meant to rotate as Shiloh's needs change (finish a unit on fractions, retire that Squire, seat a new one for the next topic).

**Two kinds of Squire, handled differently:**

- **Historical figures** — long dead, fully documented public record (Tesla, Marcus Aurelius, Euclid, etc.). Safe to have Claude role-play in full first-person voice, drawing on their real known writings and philosophy.
- **Modern living experts** — especially ones whose actual teaching lives in real online videos or courses (a specific YouTube math teacher, a specific basketball skills trainer, a specific business creator). For these, the better and more accurate approach is *not* to have Claude invent quotes as if it were that person speaking verbatim. Instead, frame the Squire as **"coached in the method of [Expert]"** — Claude acting as a guide who has studied and internalized that person's real, publicly published teaching approach, and directly referencing or quoting their actual material (a real video, article, or book Shiloh points it to) rather than fabricating dialogue attributed to a real living person.

This second category is exactly where the Phase 2 plan (Doc 05) is headed: pointing a Squire at a specific real YouTube video or course and having Claude teach from it, quiz Shiloh on it, and log the result — right now that's manual (paste a transcript or notes into the conversation), and Phase 2 automates the video-to-quiz pipeline.

## Activation Protocol — How to "Talk to" a Mentor

**Knights (full persona roleplay, appropriate for historical/fictionalized figures):**

> "You're acting as [Knight], coaching me on [quadrant topic]. Talk to me the way they'd actually talk — their tone, their real philosophy, their known advice — and help me think through [specific problem]."

**Squires — historical figure:** same pattern as above, scoped to the narrow topic.

**Squires — modern living expert (method-based, not verbatim roleplay):**

> "Act as a teacher coached in [Expert]'s method for [specific skill]. Here's [a video/transcript/notes] from them — teach me this material the way they actually teach it, and quiz me on it afterward."

**Brayer seat specifically (method-based, cross-quadrant):**

> "Act as a teacher coached in Opher Brayer's method. Take [thing I'm working on] and run it through the four foundations: what's the **pattern** here, how would I **re-design** that pattern, what's an **analogy** from something else I already know, and how do I **compose** it in real time? Then make me do it, don't just explain it."

Sessions with either tier get logged in the dashboard (Doc 04) just like any other quadrant activity: what was discussed, what Shiloh decided to do differently. A mentor conversation with no resulting action is a nice chat, not evidence of growth — the log should reflect that distinction. Squire sessions tied to a specific academic subject (Mind & Mastery) also count as evidence for Doc 03's tracker when logged with what was covered.
