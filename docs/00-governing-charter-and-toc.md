## 0. Why This Document Exists

Under Texas law (established in *TEA v. Leeper*, 1994), a homeschool is legally a private school. That means there is no external "Texas School Board" that certifies Shiloh's grade level, approves the curriculum, or grants him a diploma — **Thomas is the administrator of record**, with full legal authority to set standards, evaluate progress, and attest to completion. Nobody else signs off. That authority is real, but it's also a responsibility: this charter is how Thomas exercises it deliberately instead of informally, and how Shiloh learns — by direct experience — what it means to operate under a real governing structure with real accountability.

This charter is also, itself, the first entrepreneurship/civics lesson: a working example of how an institution writes down its own rules, how agreements get made and enforced between people, and how those rules get amended over time. Shiloh isn't just a student of this document. He's a party to it.

## 2. Mission

Two things are true at once, and neither is optional:

1. **Legal/academic:** Build a real, evidence-backed record that closes Shiloh's middle-school gaps and satisfies what Texas law actually requires of a homeschool — so the door stays open to public school, private school, dual credit, or college later, regardless of which path he ultimately takes.
2. **Holistic/human:** Help Shiloh figure out who he is and what he's for — strengths, interests, values, health, faith, and craft — with as much intention as the academics get. A kid who's "caught up" but has no sense of direction hasn't been served. Both matter, and the system is designed so they reinforce each other rather than compete for time.

---

## 3. Founding Principles

**1. Systems over willpower.** Shiloh's 7th-grade struggle wasn't a knowledge problem — teachers liked him, he showed up, and he wasn't behind because he couldn't understand the material. He fell behind because he never had a working system for managing multiple assignments, deadlines, and test prep, and once behind, the hole got psychologically bigger than it needed to be. HomeSchool OS treats that as the actual thing to fix, not a character flaw. Every quadrant includes explicit organization/planning scaffolding, not just content.

**2. Choose-your-own-adventure, inside real structure.** Shiloh has real choice over his mentors, his elective quadrant work, and how he demonstrates mastery — but the choices happen inside a defined structure with real deadlines and real consequences, because that's what he needs practice with.

**3. Evidence over assertion.** Every quadrant produces a paper trail — a project, a video, a test, a mentor sign-off, a receipt. Thomas isn't grading from memory, and if anyone ever asks "how do you know he learned this," there's an answer.

**4. Thomas is Headmaster, not just Dad, during school hours.** This is a real distinction. When acting as Headmaster, Thomas is the administrator exercising his legal authority under Leeper — and Shiloh is expected to treat the structure (deadlines, sign-offs, contracts) as real, not as a suggestion from a parent that can be negotiated the way a chore might be.

**5. Contracts are literal, not metaphorical.** Section 7 below is a real agreement both parties sign. Individual projects and privileges (see Doc 04) get their own lightweight contracts. This is deliberate — Shiloh is meant to learn how agreements are structured, what "consideration" and "breach" mean in practice, and how disputes get resolved, by living inside a system that actually uses them.

## 4. Roles

**Headmaster — Thomas.** Sets standards, approves quadrant weightings, signs off on middle-school/high-school grade designation, reviews evidence log quarterly, holds final authority on disputes.

**Student-CEO — Shiloh.** Owns his weekly plan, chooses his mentors and elective work inside each quadrant, submits evidence, runs his own dashboard. Treated as the operator of his own education, not a passive recipient of one.

**Knights (Mentors)** — see Doc 02. AI persona mentors Shiloh selects per quadrant, plus IRL mentors like Jahari. Advise and coach; they don't hold Headmaster authority.

---

## 5. How a Normal Week Runs (protocol summary — full detail in Doc 04)

1. **Monday — Planning session.** Shiloh and Thomas (or Shiloh solo, reviewed by Thomas) set the week's missions across quadrants using the dashboard.
2. **Daily — Execution + log.** Work gets done; evidence gets captured as it's produced, not reconstructed later.
3. **Friday — Review.** Evidence log updated, quadrant wheel re-scored, wins/misses discussed honestly, next week adjusted.
4. **Quarterly — Headmaster Review.** Thomas reviews the full evidence log against Doc 03's completion checklist, updates the official grade-level designation if warranted, and archives the quarter's portfolio.

---

## 6. Amendment Protocol

Any Knight, or Shiloh himself, can propose a change to any doc in this system. Changes to Docs 01, 02, 04, and 05 (the quadrants, mentors, dashboard, and roadmap) can be proposed by Shiloh and take effect once Thomas approves them — this is meant to be a living, negotiated system, not a decree handed down once.

Changes to **this document (00)** and to **Doc 03 (academic/legal compliance)** require Thomas's sign-off specifically, since those carry the legal-record and final-authority weight of the system.

All amendments get a one-line changelog entry at the bottom of the affected doc: date, what changed, who proposed it.

## 7. Founding Agreement (sample contract structure)

This is a real, functional contract template — the first one Shiloh signs, and a working model for every smaller contract this system uses later (see Doc 04 for project-level contracts).

> **HomeSchool OS Founding Charter**
>
> **Parties:** Thomas Heatherly ("Headmaster") and Shiloh Heatherly ("Student-CEO")
>
> **Term:** Effective the date signed, reviewed and renewable each academic quarter.
>
> **Headmaster's obligations (consideration):** Provide curriculum, materials, and mentor access across all quadrants; conduct weekly and quarterly reviews on schedule; give Shiloh real authority over elective choices and mentor selection as defined in Docs 01–02; maintain the evidence log and grade-level record honestly.
>
> **Student-CEO's obligations (consideration):** Complete weekly missions and submit evidence per the dashboard rhythm in Doc 04; show up to scheduled reviews prepared; communicate blockers before deadlines rather than after; treat mentor sessions and Headmaster reviews as real commitments.
>
> **Breach & remedy:** A missed weekly review or unsubmitted evidence triggers a plan-adjustment conversation, not an automatic penalty — the first response to breach is diagnosing the system failure, consistent with Principle 1. Repeated breach (defined at the next Quarterly Review) can adjust privileges tied to specific project-level contracts (Doc 04), never core provisions like mentor access.
>
> **Dispute resolution:** Either party can call a review session outside the normal schedule. Disputes not resolved between the two parties get raised with an agreed-upon third party (e.g., a Knight, or another trusted adult) before any unilateral decision is made.
>
> **Amendment:** Per Section 6 above.
>
